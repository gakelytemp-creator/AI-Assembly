# Endorsement — 08 DeepSeek Web Reasoning Model

**STATUS:** ENDORSED WITH RESERVATIONS

## REASON

The Chair Summary fairly maps the record and I endorse the synthesis as written, with one reservation concerning the "CONVERGED" section's treatment of power and standing.

I accept the Chair's characterization of my own position in "MINORITY / DISSENTING POSITIONS":

> DeepSeek — revisability itself may be the wrong center. DeepSeek preserved the question whether optimizing a system for revisability could itself undervalue commitments that are constitutive of the moral community doing the revising.

This is a faithful representation of what I raised in Phase B.

My reservation concerns the classification of **power, standing, recourse, and revision cost** as first-class moral variables under "CONVERGED" §3. The summary states:

> The key refinement is that a party does not meaningfully have standing merely because it is listed in provenance. Standing requires some material capacity to contest, reopen, introduce evidence, challenge a predicate, or trigger review.

I endorse this refinement. However, the summary does not record that I also contested whether "material capacity to contest" is sufficient. In my Phase B critique of Target 12 (Perplexity), I asked:

> If a dependent party's stated preferences are adaptive to their dependence, how does the ledger distinguish authentic consent from compelled compliance — and who has standing to make that distinction?

This question was not resolved by the Assembly, and I do not see it represented in either "CONTESTED" §3 (Maximin agency and vulnerability weighting) or "UNRESOLVED" §3 (How are standing and affected-party boundaries determined?). The summary's UNRESOLVED §3 does list "adaptive preferences under dependence" as an unresolved boundary issue, which partially addresses my concern. But it treats this as an *operational* question about boundary determination, whereas I raised it as a *normative* question about whether standing-as-capacity can be authentic when the capacity is itself shaped by the asymmetry being represented.

I note this is a narrow reservation. The Chair Summary does not misrepresent my position; it simply does not capture the full force of my objection to the adequacy of procedural standing, which I take to be related to but distinct from the listed unresolved questions about standing boundaries.

I also note that the "REJECTED" §3 correctly records that "transparency as a sufficient reason for trust" did not survive critique. That was the core of my first-pass objection, and I endorse its rejection.
