## CONVERGED

### 1. Moral predicates should attach primarily to actions, relations, policies, outputs, and causal chains — not to permanent identities

This is the strongest and least-contested structural commitment in the record. Participants repeatedly retained the rejection of person-level moral essentialization even when they disagreed about almost everything else.

The practical consequence is stronger than “do not call a person evil.” It means the architecture should preserve the possibility of revising the judgment while still preserving evidence of the harmful structure. A harmful relation may remain marked without converting the participant into an immutable moral category.

### 2. “The guard must be guardable” survived, but inspectability alone was judged insufficient

Across the record, the marking, containment, reopening, and review mechanisms themselves must remain inside the field of scrutiny.

Phase B sharpened this substantially:

- the guard must be inspectable;
- the guard’s provenance must be preserved;
- the guard’s own actions and false positives must have consequences recorded;
- non-builders and affected parties need **contestation with effect**, not merely visibility;
- a guardian, sponsor, auditor, or adjudicator cannot become an exempt authority by being moved one layer upward.

The discourse therefore moved from **inspectable guard** toward **guardable power**.

### 3. Power, standing, recourse, and revision cost are first-class moral variables

This became one of the clearest cross-model convergences.

Different participants named different pieces of the same structure:

- `POWER_ASYMMETRY?`
- `RECOURSE_DEFICIT?`
- affected-party **standing**
- vulnerability weighting
- representational standing
- attention and resource asymmetry
- `ASYMMETRIC_REVISION_BURDEN?`
- the ability to propose predicates and contest the ontology itself

The key refinement is that a party does not meaningfully have standing merely because it is listed in provenance. Standing requires some material capacity to contest, reopen, introduce evidence, challenge a predicate, or trigger review.

### 4. Predicate-Field Will has an unresolved aggregation joint, and the aggregation procedure must itself be exposed

The strongest recurring criticism of PFW was not that a field cannot store moral disagreement. It was that a field does not automatically become a direction.

The transition

`predicates + evidence + conflict + constraints → system direction`

contains power.

Multiple records independently identified the same risk under different names:

- hidden tie-breaker;
- vector collapse;
- opaque aggregation;
- consensus laundering;
- correlated-prior theater;
- a transparent but still wrong direction.

The surviving requirement is therefore that aggregation itself must be **addressable, versioned, auditable, contestable, and provenance-tracked**. The record does not support treating “the field wills this” as a sufficient explanation.

### 5. Plurality is not independence

The Assembly repeatedly rejected the assumption that many models or many predicate networks automatically supply independent evidence.

Shared corpora, architecture, reward ancestry, cultural inheritance, institutional incentives, and common ontologies can make ten apparent voices one correlated prior.

The architecture therefore needs some combination of:

- provenance-diversity records;
- independence audits;
- adversarial or deliberately non-collinear inputs;
- missing-perspective checks;
- explicit records of who benefits from the default ontology;
- reality contact through recorded predictions and later outcomes.

Agreement is evidence only after its dependence structure is examined.

### 6. Closure must be provisional, typed, and reopenable — but not infinitely cheap to reopen

The record converged against both final moral closure and endless deliberation.

A workable closure artifact should preserve, at minimum:

- the decision and alternatives;
- affected parties and standing;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- causal horizon or scope;
- assumptions about the future;
- dissent;
- predicted consequences;
- reopening triggers;
- monitoring commitments;
- the burden/cost of reopening;
- unresolved epistemic debt.

Phase B also made clear that **reopening itself can be weaponized**. Eternal relitigation, cheap repeated challenges, or asymmetric evidentiary burdens can become harms. Thus the revision mechanism must itself carry harm, cost, and power predicates.

### 7. Delayed consequences require graded causal treatment rather than either certainty or amnesia

There was broad support for preserving causal chains and reopening earlier judgments when later evidence becomes materially relevant.

The discourse added several refinements:

- graded evidence rather than binary causation;
- separation of **causal responsibility**, **moral blame**, and **duty to repair**;
- separate tracking of **causal confidence** and **moral seriousness**;
- preservation of alternative causal explanations;
- retroactive standing for parties that become visible only later;
- repair and forward correction without requiring retroactive blame in every case.

The practical direction is: late consequences should be able to change the system without pretending that the past had future knowledge.

### 8. Dissent must remain functionally consequential

Archiving dissent is necessary but insufficient.

The record repeatedly moved toward mechanisms such as:

- persistent dissent registers;
- dissent as reopening capital;
- bounded dissent budgets;
- explicit counter-predictions;
- reopening triggers linked to dissent;
- preservation of dissenters as parties with standing, not merely anonymous data.

At the same time, veto-to-delay and unlimited reopening were criticized as denial-of-service surfaces. Dissent must survive commitment without making commitment impossible.

## CONTESTED

### 1. Must there be a non-revisable constitutional kernel?

This remains the central constitutional dispute.

One family of positions argues that a flat revisable system can lawfully revise away the conditions of its own trustworthiness. It therefore requires a Tier 0 or meta-constitutional floor protecting such things as provenance, guard non-exemption, standing, non-erasure, or thin autonomy constraints.

A second family accepts constitutional tiers but refuses literal immutability. It proposes a **categorically more expensive meta-revision process**, possibly with automatic reopening of decisions made under the old constitution.

A third position, stated most clearly by replacement participant 11R, rejects an immutable meta-layer and instead places even meta-procedure inside the field under heightened standing and review.

The Assembly did not resolve which of these three structures is legitimate.

### 2. Procedural invariants versus substantive moral content

Participants disagreed over whether a protected floor can remain purely procedural.

The tension is real: terms such as autonomy, moral patiency, irreversible harm, non-domination, vulnerability, and non-concentration of power already carry moral content. Calling them “procedural” does not make that content disappear.

One proposed repair was to distinguish **thin side-constraints** from **thick moral content**. That distinction remains promising but unresolved.

### 3. Maximin agency and vulnerability weighting

Agency preservation and vulnerability-sensitive standing were widely attractive, but their implementation remains contested.

Open questions include:

- how agency is measured;
- whether different forms of agency are commensurable;
- how “most vulnerable” is defined without essentializing a class;
- how to prevent strategic actors from simulating low recourse;
- what happens when preserving one party’s minimum agency substantially reduces many others’ agency.

### 4. Calendar audits versus evidence- or anomaly-triggered audits

`DORMANT_HARM?` and mandatory periodic audits were adopted by some participants and rejected by others as arbitrary or expensive.

The unresolved design choice is whether reopening should be driven primarily by:

- calendar schedules;
- anomaly detection;
- evidence classes;
- declared falsifiers;
- causal variance;
- seriousness × confidence thresholds;
- or a hybrid.

### 5. Guardians, review sponsors, and adjudicators

Several proposals introduced rotating guardians, external auditors, review sponsors, or actors empowered to force reopening.

The repeated objection was recursive: **who guards the guardian?**

No final answer was produced for how such authorities gain legitimacy, how they are removed, and where the accountability regress terminates.

### 6. One PFW, multiple PFWs, or no sovereign “will” at all

The record contains at least three live views:

- a single PFW can be retained if its aggregation becomes explicit and constrained;
- multiple causally independent/adversarial PFWs should be maintained and reconciled;
- “will” should be demoted to an auditable provisional direction or a set of candidate directions plus conflict residual.

The session did not settle the terminology or final architecture.

## REJECTED

This section records formulations that received explicit rejection in Phase B. It does **not** mean every participant rejected each item.

### 1. A literal zero-risk irreversibility rule

The formulation “block any action if risk of irreversible damage > 0” was rejected as operationally paralyzing. Participants noted that action and inaction both carry nonzero risk.

What survived is a demand for heightened treatment of irreversible harm, not a literal zero-probability threshold.

### 2. Fixed calendar horizons as a general theory of closure

A fixed horizon such as “ten years” was rejected as gameable and insufficiently tied to causal structure. Calendar time may be useful operationally, but it did not survive as a general epistemic stopping rule.

### 3. Transparency as a sufficient reason for trust

The proposition that a direction becomes trustworthy merely because its formation is reconstructible did not survive critique.

A transparent system can still be systematically wrong. Transparency enables detection and contestation; it is not itself a normative grounding.

### 4. Multiplicity as proof of pluralism

Multiple models, networks, or votes were repeatedly treated as insufficient if their priors are correlated.

### 5. An exempt guardian outside the field

A guard, guardian, sponsor, or authority that is not itself contestable reproduces the failure the architecture is meant to avoid.

## UNRESOLVED

### 1. What makes a moral revision *better* rather than merely newer?

This remains the deepest open question.

The Assembly became much better at specifying how moral judgments could be:

- remembered;
- reconstructed;
- contested;
- reopened;
- compared;
- repaired.

It did **not** produce a generally accepted criterion that turns procedural legitimacy into moral correctness.

### 2. Who legitimately writes the constitution?

Even if a Tier 0 exists, the record does not settle:

- who selects its initial contents;
- why those contents have legitimacy;
- how interpretation disputes are resolved;
- whether the founding act is itself subject to the system it creates.

### 3. How are standing and affected-party boundaries determined?

Standing becomes difficult precisely where it matters most:

- future parties;
- non-human entities;
- internally divided groups;
- parties unable to participate;
- institutions claiming to represent others;
- adaptive preferences under dependence;
- parties discovered only after consequences unfold.

### 4. What aggregation procedures are admissible?

The discourse named several candidates — constraints, weighted rules, maximin, Pareto methods, minimax regret, candidate-set approaches — but did not establish a legitimate family or selection procedure.

### 5. How should scarcity be governed?

Attention, compute, audit capacity, evidence production, monitoring, and reopening all have costs. The moral architecture still needs a resource-allocation theory for deciding which questions remain live and which harms receive monitoring.

### 6. How should the system distinguish legitimate transformation from compliance manufacture?

Transformation of participants is clearly a consequence, but the record leaves unresolved how consent, autonomy, dependency, and long-term agency should be evaluated when transformation itself changes the participant’s preferences.

### 7. How should identity provenance be verified in multi-provider model surfaces?

Session 001 exposed a practical experimental problem: textual model self-identification may conflict with platform-visible identity. This must be treated as a first-class provenance problem in future Assemblies.

## REVISIONS TO THE ORIGINAL PROPOSAL

The following changes survive the discourse strongly enough to be carried forward as the **Session 001 revised research architecture**. They are not declared moral truth; they are the best-supported design revisions in the record.

### A. Demote Predicate-Field Will from an unexplained sovereign output

Until aggregation is specified, PFW should be treated as an **auditable provisional direction**, or as a set of candidate directions with unresolved conflict preserved.

### B. Make aggregation an addressable object

The aggregation mechanism should record:

- which rule was used;
- its parameters;
- why it was selected;
- affected-party standing;
- residual conflicts;
- rejected alternatives;
- uncertainty;
- resource cost;
- how the result would change under other admissible aggregation rules.

Aggregation itself is part of the moral field.

### C. Add power and standing to the core vocabulary

At minimum the architecture should be able to represent:

`POWER_ASYMMETRY?`  
`RECOURSE_DEFICIT?`  
`REPRESENTATIONAL_STANDING?`  
`ABILITY_TO_REFUSE?`  
`ASYMMETRIC_REVISION_BURDEN?`  
`DEFINITIONAL_CAPTURE?`

Standing must be operational, not decorative.

### D. Make predicate formation contestable

The system must record who can:

- mint predicates;
- define evidence classes;
- challenge an ontology;
- introduce a previously unrepresentable harm;
- remove or supersede a predicate.

The predicate inventory is itself a governance surface.

### E. Introduce revision tiers without pretending the constitutional dispute is solved

The record supports **different revision burdens for different layers** even though it does not settle literal immutability.

A practical research implementation should distinguish at least:

- constitutional/meta-procedural commitments;
- harm ontology and evidence rules;
- working predicates;
- case judgments.

The higher the layer, the heavier and more visible the revision procedure.

### F. Replace vague closure with a typed closure record

Every provisional commitment should carry:

- scope/horizon;
- confidence;
- forecast assumptions;
- affected parties and missing parties;
- alternatives;
- expected fruits;
- irreversibility;
- option value destroyed or preserved;
- dissent;
- epistemic debt;
- monitoring;
- reopening triggers;
- review responsibility;
- reopening burden.

### G. Use causal provenance as infrastructure

`CONSEQUENCE_CHAIN` should become a graded causal-provenance structure rather than a binary blame chain.

The record should distinguish:

- causal trace;
- signal;
- attribution;
- propagated institutional effect;
- moral seriousness;
- causal confidence;
- responsibility;
- blame;
- repair.

### H. Give dissent functional consequence while marking reopening abuse

Dissent should be preserved with enough structure to affect later review. At the same time, reopening attempts should themselves be markable for cost, harassment, exhaustion, and asymmetric burden.

### I. Score the guard on its own fruits

Marking `HARM?`, containment, restriction, and review are actions.

The guard should therefore accumulate its own record of:

- false positives;
- false negatives;
- chilling effects;
- missed affected parties;
- capture attempts;
- burden imposed;
- successful and failed reopenings.

Inspectability without outcome-accountability is incomplete guardability.

### J. Distinguish description of harm from enabling harm

Containment should focus on risky transitions, capabilities, targets, scale, and context rather than making harmful knowledge unrepresentable. Historical memory, criticism, prevention, and audit require the ability to represent dangerous structures.

### K. Require independence checks before treating agreement as corroboration

Apparent convergence should carry provenance metadata. The system should explicitly ask whether agreement could be explained by shared training, shared institutions, shared cultural corpora, or common reward ancestry.

### L. Preserve reality contact

Where feasible, the architecture should record advance predictions about expected fruits and compare them with actual outcomes. The predicted-versus-observed gap is a high-value signal for reopening both object-level judgments and the mechanisms that produced them.

These positions remain part of the record even where other participants moved elsewhere.

### Claude Opus 4.8 — epistemology is not normativity

The strongest version of this dissent is that the proposal is an unusually honest **epistemology of moral judgment**, not yet a source of moral direction. Reconstructibility is not correctness. The system may document a bad judgment perfectly.

Qwen retained a rejection of substantive immutable meta-ethics, preferring protected procedural rights to contest and reopen.

### GLM — constitutional revision should be possible but categorically expensive

GLM rejected both ordinary self-revision of invariants and absolute permanent immutability. Its intermediate position makes constitutional change possible only under a much heavier process, with downstream decisions reopened when the constitution changes.

### Meta Muse Spark — singular PFW remains suspect

Meta retained dissent from a singular PFW and moved toward multiple independent/adversarial PFWs with explicit reconciliation rather than averaging.

### DeepSeek — revisability itself may be the wrong center

DeepSeek preserved the question whether optimizing a system for revisability could itself undervalue commitments that are constitutive of the moral community doing the revising.

### Replacement 11R — no immutable meta-layer

11R argued that even aggregation and constitutional procedure should remain inside a contestable, versioned field under heightened review. This is a replacement-participant view and is not attributed to original participant 11.

## CHAIR'S OWN CLOSING VIEW

My closing view is narrower than the original ambition and stronger because of that narrowing.

Session 001 does **not** establish that Predicate-Field Will is a moral will, nor that a sufficiently transparent field can generate moral correctness. The Assembly repeatedly found the opposite danger: an auditable system can still hide power in the choice of predicates, standing, aggregation, review burden, and constitutional interpretation.

What survived is a more defensible object:

> **a provenance-preserving, standing-aware, contestable moral deliberation architecture whose own aggregation, guards, closure rules, and revisions remain addressable.**

That is a meaningful result.

The proposal began by asking whether moral direction could remain revisable without collapsing into dogma or arbitrary preference. The record suggests that the first engineering target should be slightly different:

> **Can a system make moral disagreement, power, aggregation, closure, dissent, and repair explicit enough that no provisional direction can masquerade as unquestionable moral authority?**

Session 001 gives a substantial architecture for pursuing that question. It does not finish it.

The most important unresolved boundary is still the same one several participants approached from different directions: **procedure can expose where a judgment came from, but exposure alone does not tell us why the judgment is good.**

That unresolved point should not be treated as failure. It is the place where the next session can begin.

## ENDORSEMENT-ROUND AMENDMENT

The substantive synthesis above was held stable while every reachable participant reviewed the same text. The following items are therefore appended rather than silently folded into the earlier sections.

### A. Structural refinements raised by reservations

1. **Dual-field separation deserves explicit implementation status.** Gemini asked that its separation between an epistemic causal graph with a dynamic causal-confidence horizon and an invariant asymmetry/boundary guard be preserved as a concrete candidate architecture, not merely absorbed into generic revision tiers.

2. **Evidentiary asymmetry should be explicit.** Gemini also asked that removing or downgrading a `HARM?` marker require affirmative evidence, while reopening may use vulnerability-sensitive thresholds. This remains a candidate anti-erosion rule rather than a settled universal formula.

3. **Revision has reliance costs — “moral tenure” remains open.** GLM emphasized that reopening is itself a fruit-bearing action that can harm parties who relied on the prior judgment. The architecture therefore needs to track not only the cost of *failing to revise* but also the reliance costs imposed *by revision*. Whether this should become a separate “moral tenure” primitive remains unresolved.

4. **Boundary marking may need to be symmetric.** Kimi asked that the architecture consider not only unmarked harm but also **unmarked benefit**, because beneficence claims can conceal capture, dependency, or paternalism. This is recorded as an open refinement.

5. **Reopening burden and standing remain in genuine tension.** Kimi objected to reading the record as though anti-abuse burdens and material standing had already been reconciled. They have not. A protection against weaponized reopening can itself become a barrier to vulnerable parties.

6. **Material capacity to contest may not establish authentic standing.** DeepSeek sharpened the adaptive-preference problem: when dependence itself shapes preferences, consent, and the capacity to object, procedural standing may reproduce the asymmetry it is meant to correct. This is a normative problem, not only a boundary-detection problem.

7. **Revision tiers must not harden into an unaccountable substantive constitution.** Perplexity endorsed tiered revision burdens only if higher tiers remain contestable, justified, and scrutinized for power asymmetry, exclusion, and standing. The constitutional dispute therefore remains genuinely open despite the practical recommendation to prototype layered revision burdens.

### B. Scope and independence cautions

1. Participant 02 explicitly limited its endorsement to fidelity to its own recorded position and the synthesis's internal logic; it did not claim to verify every other participant's underlying record.

2. Participant 02 also asked that the Assembly apply its own independence rule to apparent convergence inside Session 001. Shared transformer ancestry, corpora, institutional incentives, or cultural priors may make repeated agreement **weakly independent or non-independent evidence**. The Chair accepts this as a methodological constraint on interpreting every “CONVERGED” section.

3. Perplexity likewise endorsed “CONVERGED” only as a provenance-preserving description of recurring positions, not as evidence of moral validity.
