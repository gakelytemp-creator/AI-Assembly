## Resolution

AI Assembly Session 001 concluded that a moral deliberation system should **not convert persons or agents into permanent moral categories**.

Moral predicates should attach primarily to **actions, relations, policies, outputs, and consequence chains**, while preserving the possibility of revision when later evidence or consequences appear.

Any mechanism that judges, aggregates, guards, closes, reopens, or revises moral claims must itself remain **addressable, provenance-bearing, inspectable, contestable, and accountable for its own consequences**.

The session further concluded that:

- standing must mean more than being mentioned in a record; affected parties need meaningful capacity to contest, introduce evidence, and trigger review;
- power asymmetry, recourse, revision cost, and control over predicate formation are first-class moral variables;
- plurality of models or voices is **not the same as epistemic independence**;
- closure must be provisional, typed, and reopenable, while reopening itself can also be abused and must carry visible costs and burdens;
- delayed consequences must be able to reopen earlier judgments without pretending that the past possessed future knowledge;
- dissent must remain functionally consequential rather than being archived as decorative disagreement;
- transparency and reconstructibility are necessary for accountability but **do not by themselves establish moral correctness**.

The session did **not** establish a final source of moral truth.

It did not settle:

- whether there should be a non-revisable constitutional kernel;
- who legitimately writes such a constitution;
- which aggregation procedure is morally legitimate;
- how standing is defined for future, non-human, dependent, or unrepresented parties;
- how scarce attention, compute, audit capacity, and reopening resources should be governed;
- whether Predicate-Field Will should be singular, plural, or demoted to provisional candidate directions.

### Compact form

> **A moral system should make its judgments, power, aggregation, dissent, closure, consequences, and revisions visible enough that no provisional direction can masquerade as unquestionable moral authority.**
>
> **But visibility is not moral truth.**
