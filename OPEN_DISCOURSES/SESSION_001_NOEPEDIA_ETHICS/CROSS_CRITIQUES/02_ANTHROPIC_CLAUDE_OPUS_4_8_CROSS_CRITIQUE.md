**Revision of my own first-pass position**

It changes — not in thesis, but in several load-bearing specifics. Honest bookkeeping, separated into what held, what moved, what I got wrong, and what I add.

Survived unchanged:

- The central mislabeling — an honest epistemology of moral judgment is being presented as a source of moral direction; reconstructibility is not correctness; procedural virtue cannot generate normativity. Unrefuted across all three. Meta's replacement question ("don't give it a will; make its influence inspectable, contestable, reversible") converges on it from a different door.
- The self-abolition diagnosis — a fully flat revisable system can lawfully revise away the conditions of its own trustworthiness, every step audited and clean. This remained distinctive; Meta's "may not make future revision impossible" is the *fix* I proposed, arrived at independently, but the *diagnosis of why the fix is load-bearing* is still mine and stands.
- The scored guard — the act of marking is itself a fruit-bearing action, so the marking machinery must be scored on its own false positives and chilling effects, not merely inspected. All three wanted the guard inspectable; none stated the recursion. It survives, and I now hold it is strictly stronger than the "inspectable guard" the other three settled for.

Moved / upgraded:

1. Power/standing goes from "standing to reopen" to **contestation-with-effect by non-builders** (Meta). Inspectability was never the operational meaning of "guardable."
2. The untracked power is now **located concretely in the aggregation step** (Grok, Cohere). The predicates-to-PFW weighting function is where it hides, so that function — not just the field — must carry power markers and be contestable.
3. **Non-concentration of irreversible power** is promoted from a *tracked object* to a **Tier-0 invariant** (Meta). Tracking concentration does not prevent it; cap it.

Got wrong, and repair:

4. I wrote that the invariant "cannot be a specific moral content — that is dogma," then placed an autonomy floor — specific content — in Tier 0 and called it "procedure." That was inconsistent, and Cohere and Meta both fixing substantive floors is what forced me to see it. The correct line, which I should have drawn in Phase A: distinguish **thin side-constraints** — what may not be done to anyone regardless of the resulting good (autonomy floor, avoid-irreversible-catastrophe, non-concentration-of-power) — from **thick content** — which acts and ends are good. Thin side-constraints can be fixed without being dogma; thick content must stay revisable. My first-pass claim "no substantive content" was overstated; the accurate claim is "no thick content, a small set of thin side-constraints." I concede the error rather than paper it.

Added:

5. A **moral-patiency schema** (Cohere), specifying whom and what the floors protect — but placed at **Tier 1 (hard to revise), not Tier 2 (free)**, precisely so the protected set cannot be hollowed by back-door redefinition. This is the same objection I posed to Cohere; because it applies to my own architecture, I answer it inside the architecture instead of leaving it open.
6. **Consent to transformation** (Meta). Section 9's transformation now carries `HARM?` *and* a requirement that the transformed party endorse it. "A more responsible participant" is benign only if consented; unconsented, it is paternalistic shaping, and from the outside it is indistinguishable from the manufacture of obedience.
7. **Option value, epistemic commons, and incentive effects as explicit fruits** (Meta). My praise for "change the board" had no hook; the fruits-list must count effects on the future option set and incentive structure, or judge-by-fruits registers only realized outcomes and misses the board-change — the very thing I called the system's best ambition.

What held and why: the diagnosis, the self-abolition invariant, and the scored-guard recursion are the parts no participant refuted or matched, and they carry the weight. The seven amendments improve the *mechanism* without touching the *claim*.

Visibility is what lets you *catch* a bad will; it is never a reason to *trust* one. That was my opening sentence in Phase A and it leaves Phase B intact.
