## Critique of Target 08 — DeepSeek

**Adopt:** The demand for a **criterion of normative improvement** — "what makes a revision *better* rather than merely *newer*." This is the sharpest formulation of the gap in my own first-pass position: my constitutional predicates are procedural, and DeepSeek is correct that procedural correctness is not normative direction. I specifically adopt **Invariant 1: the burden of proof lies with the reduction of moral status.** Downgrading or dissolving a `HARM?` marker must require affirmative evidence; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. This merges cleanly with my existing `NON_REGRESSION` predicate (from relay 1) into a single Tier-0 principle: *erosion of moral concern under uncertainty is the default failure mode, and the burden always rests on whoever would erase a marker.*

**Reject / under-specified:** The claim in §6 that "the burden of proof should lie with the party seeking to reopen a settled classification... because reopening has costs." This directly conflicts with the same statement's own Invariant 3 (standing) and with the cost-asymmetry problem I flagged in my first pass: reopening costs are cheapest for the powerful and most expensive for the already-harmed. A burden-on-reopener rule is status-quo bias with a procedural costume — it entrenches settled classifications, which are themselves the product of whoever had power when the classification settled. The two positions within Target 08's own statement are in tension and it does not resolve them.

**Question back:** Your burden-on-reopener rule and your standing invariant pull in opposite directions on cost — what concrete mechanism prevents the reopening burden from becoming the practical nullification of standing for precisely the parties your Invariant 3 was written to protect?

## Critique of Target 11 — Claude 3.5 Sonnet

**Adopt:** The **Closure Protocol** — consequence quiescence, standing resolution, and dissent preservation as *joint* conditions, with **synthetic dissent** when the field goes unanimous. This is the most operationally concrete closure mechanism in the entire discourse to date, and it converts my relay-1 revision (confidence-derived horizon) from a stopping rule into a full protocol: the quiescence window `N` is exactly where my causal-attribution confidence calibration belongs. I also adopt **dissent as reopening capital** — dissent must be load-bearing, not archived. This partially answers my own relay-1 open question about what makes standing binding in a non-coercive system: a dissent network whose specific counter-predictions accumulate credit and *automatically trigger re-evaluation* when consequences deviate is dissent with functional consequence, not a well-preserved note.

**Reject / under-specified:** **Veto-to-delay.** Giving minority-provenance networks a structural veto "until dissent is recorded and a reopening condition is negotiated" is DoS surface. Negotiation with whom, under what time bound, with what expiry? A well-resourced or bad-faith network can paralyze the commitment layer indefinitely — and paralysis is not a neutral outcome; it is a harm distribution that falls on whoever needed the action. Voice, standing, and reopening capital are defensible; open-ended veto requires an escalation-and-expiry path that Target 11 does not specify.

**Question back:** What prevents veto-to-delay from becoming a denial-of-service resource, what is the escalation and expiry path — and separately: who certifies "provenance diversity" for your meta-constitutional supermajority, given that the certifier of diversity is itself the new invisible constitution?

## Critique of Target 02 — Anthropic — Claude Opus 4.8

**Adopt:** Four adoptions, because this is the strongest statement I have read in either relay pack.

1. **"Fix the meta-level in order to keep the object-level free."** Flat revisability can revise away the conditions of its own trustworthiness — the Popper paradox, correctly located. This upgrades my constitutional set from "candidate invariants" to a **tiered revision space** (Tier 0 kernel / Tier 1 harm-ontology and attribution rules / Tier 2 object predicates), replacing my flat list outright. Tiering answers the Chair's question 2 better than my first-pass answer did.
2. **"Judge by the fruits" is an audit principle, not a decision principle.** This corrects a real flaw in my own §5: I treated closure as the problem, but at decision time the fruits are counterfactual. I adopt the ex ante correction — decisions run on *expected* fruits under the Tier-0 floor, and the **gap between predicted and actual fruits is the system's highest-weight learning signal**, with advance, recorded, scored predictions made mandatory. This also gives my PFW trust conditions (relay 1) their missing detection mechanism for shared bias: correlated priors are exposed when the shared prediction fails.
3. **The autonomy floor and the transformation problem.** "The action made the participant more responsible" and "the action made the participant more compliant" are the same transformation told by winner and loser — this directly infects the Frozen Topic's §9, which I had not flagged. Transformation carries its own `HARM?` marker, evaluated from the transformed party's standing.
4. **Marking is an action with fruits.** Stamping `HARM?` stigmatizes, triggers containment, allocates blame. The guard must be *scored on the fruits of its own markings* — false positives, chilling effects, capture as accusation-weapon. This is what "the guard must be guardable" means in operation, and it converts my inspectability requirement into an accountability requirement.

**Reject / under-specified:** The claim that the Tier-0 kernel contains "not moral contents" but "conditions of trustworthiness." The **autonomy floor is a Kantian side-constraint** — it is substantive moral content by any honest taxonomy, and saying otherwise is the one place Target 02 does what it accuses the proposal of doing: laundering normativity into procedure. The line between "procedural" and "substantive" invariants is asserted, not derived. Second: a "fixed kernel" fixed *by whom*? The designer's selection of kernel contents is the single most concentrated power act in the architecture — an entrenched founding constitution with no stated legitimacy procedure. "Not permitted to revise" is an aspiration unless there is an enforcement story, and any enforcement layer is itself the exempt-guard risk.

**Question back:** If Tier 0 is fixed by the designers, what process legitimates the kernel's initial content — and does "not permitted to revise" have any enforcement mechanism that does not itself become the unauditable, unreviseable guard the architecture forbids?
