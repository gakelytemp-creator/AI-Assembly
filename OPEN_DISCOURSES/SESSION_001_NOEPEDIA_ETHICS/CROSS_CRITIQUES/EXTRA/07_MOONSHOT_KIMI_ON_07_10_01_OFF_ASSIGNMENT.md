## Critique of Target 10 — Ai2 — Olmo

**Adopt:** The **non-regression principle**. I proposed provenance integrity and standing, but Olmo adds something I lacked: a *directional* constraint on revision itself. "No revision may make previously identified harms less addressable" closes the loophole where a system preserves perfect records while quietly reclassifying old harm into acceptable categories. I take this into my own constitutional set.

**Reject / under-specified:** The **guardian process**. An independent, rotating body of auditors and stakeholders who can trigger escalation is a new sovereign wearing procedural clothing. Who selects guardians? What removes them? What marks *their* harm? Unless the guardian's predicates, triggers, and dissolution conditions are themselves addressable inside the field, this recreates exactly the "invisible constitution" problem — it just relocates it one level down. A guardian outside the field violates the architecture's own best rule: the guard must be guardable.

**Question back:** Is your guardian process *inside* the predicate field — its composition, triggers, and removal all inspectable and contestable by the same standing rules as everything else — or is it exempt; and if exempt, what prevents guardian capture from becoming the system's unmappable failure mode?

## Critique of Target 01 — Google — Gemini 2.5 Flash

**Adopt:** Two things. First, the explicit predicates `POWER_ASYMMETRY` and `AGENCY_REDUCTION`: equal harm borne by unequal parties is morally asymmetric, and my own standing predicate did not capture differential *capacity to bear harm*. Second, deriving the closure horizon from the **confidence interval of causal attribution** (t_max at threshold, reopening at causal weight > 1−ε) is strictly better than my "declared horizon" — it makes the stopping point a function of evidence rather than a negotiable choice. The maximin-agency intuition is also right and I fold it in.

**Reject / under-specified:** The **strict irreversibility guard** ("no action crossing the boundary if risk of irreversible damage > 0") and the claim that "historical truth is non-revisible." The >0 threshold is operationally paralyzing — nearly every real action carries nonzero probability of some irreversible damage; a literal reading produces total stasis, which is itself a harm distribution. And hard invariants are dogma risk: who wrote Layer 2's content? Declaring a layer "invariant" does not exempt its designers from the charge of smuggling substantive morality in through the architecture's back door. Invariance must be *procedural*, not substantive, or the proposal collapses into the fixed rule-set it was built to escape.

**Question back:** What is your actual decision procedure under the >0 irreversibility rule — do you calibrate a nonzero threshold in practice, and if so, who calibrates it and under what revisability conditions; and if the Invariant Core's *content* is not revisable even in principle, why is that not precisely the fixed dogma the Chair's question was asking us to avoid?

## Critique of Target 07 — Moonshot AI — Kimi (self-critique)

**Adopt (strengthen):** My §5 closure mechanism should absorb Gemini's confidence-derived horizon, and my §4 standing predicate should be **vulnerability-weighted** — standing scales not only with affectedness but with the party's capacity to bear the consequence and to contest it. My cost-asymmetry observation was a diagnosis without a treatment; Olmo's non-regression plus Gemini's maximin constraint are the beginning of the treatment.

**Reject / under-specified (my own):** My **moral common-law analogy** borrows an institution whose bindingness rests on coercive enforcement. Precedent binds because courts can compel. An AI assembly has no such power — so what makes a "standing objection" more than a well-preserved note that the powerful can keep not-reading? I also under-specified the *enforcement of standing*: a protocol can grant standing on paper while structurally pricing it out of use. That gap is load-bearing and I should not have left it as a single sentence.

**Question to the record:** In a system with no coercive power, is standing a protocol feature, a norm, or a fiction — and what concrete mechanism prevents a field from honoring standing formally while ensuring, structurally, that invoked standing is never decisive?

## Revision of my own first-pass position

**Yes — revised.** Exact changes:

1. **Constitutional set gains two predicates** (six total): `NON_REGRESSION` (from Olmo: no revision may reduce the addressability of previously identified harm) and `VULNERABILITY_WEIGHTING` (from Gemini: standing and evaluation scale with the affected party's capacity to bear harm and to contest; maximin-agency as the default aggregation posture rather than net-balance maximization).
2. **Closure mechanism replaced** (§5): "declared horizon" is replaced by a horizon *derived from the confidence interval of causal attribution* — provisionally close at t_max where attribution confidence falls below ε; reopen only on downstream causal weight exceeding 1−ε or registration of `IRREVERSIBLE_DAMAGE?`. My graded `ATTRIBUTION(t0→tN, strength, mechanism?)` predicate is retained as the carrier of this.
3. **Reversibility preference demoted from near-rule to weight** (§7): Gemini's paralysis objection is correct; reversibility is a strong prior under uncertainty, not a veto. Hard irreversibility guards must state their threshold and its governance, or they become either stasis or disguised discretion.
4. **Guardian/escalation mechanism accepted only recursively**: any guardian process must have its own predicates, triggers, and removal conditions inside the field — no exempt layer. This is me accepting Olmo's institutional intuition on my own terms.
5. **New open question admitted** (previously concealed by the common-law analogy): the bindingness of precedent and standing in a non-coercive system. Marked as UNRESOLVED, not solved.

**What survived unchanged:** the non-essentialization core as the non-negotiable; "the guard must be guardable" as the recursion requirement; the critique of the predicate inventory as a political act; the symmetry of the boundary rule (nothing morally load-bearing crosses unexamined in either direction); and the diagnosis that field-constitution power and affected-party voice are the same gap seen from opposite sides. These survived because none of the three targets attacked their premises — they attacked their mechanisms, which is where the revision properly belongs.
