**Target 12 — Perplexity (model undisclosed)**

**Adopt** — two things, one with a modification.

The load-bearing one: *"No materially affected party may be rendered morally invisible by the system's representation scheme."* This sits upstream of everything I built. My standing mechanism — sharpened from Meta into contestation-with-effect by non-builders — presupposes a party is already in the ledger and asks whether it can force reconsideration. 12 names the prior gate: the representation scheme itself can erase a party before any question of contestation arises, and an action can be fully marked, fully provenanced, fully auditable, and still unjust because the cost-bearers were never entered as parties at all. That is a real hole in my architecture and I take it: it adds a required ledger field — who was *not* represented, and why — and makes representational completeness a first-class check rather than an afterthought.

I adopt it with one modification, because 12's rule as literally stated is unsatisfiable and collides with 12's own anti-omniscience stance. If consequences arrive late through chains invisible at t0 — which both 12 and the proposal insist on — then at the moment of action the representation scheme necessarily renders some future-affected parties invisible, not by injustice but by epistemic limit. A rule forbidding *all* invisibility demands the foresight 12 elsewhere denies ("this is not infinite foresight"). So the rule must be indexed to foreseeability plus a retroactive-standing mechanism: no *foreseeable* affected party may be rendered invisible, the scheme must declare its own foreseeability horizon, and a party made visible only at tN must be able to acquire standing retroactively and reopen. That is compatible with the evidence-indexed reopening I already hold, and it turns 12's principle from an unsatisfiable absolute into an operational one.

The second adoption: 12's decoupling of causal confidence from moral seriousness into two independent axes, with the rule that high seriousness lowers the causal-confidence bar for reopening while a minor effect demands stronger attribution. This sharpens my "closed conditional on stated reopening triggers" — the triggers should be indexed on both axes jointly, not on evidence-strength alone. Taken.

**Reject / under-specified.** 12's central and correct move is that power asymmetry is the missing variable — it converges with my own first-pass claim almost to the phrasing (12: mechanisms that "prevent revision from becoming mere power"; me: conditions "under which moral claims can be trusted to track truth rather than power"). But 12 applies the diagnosis to the proposal and not to its own remedies. 12's boundary rule installs an "accountable sponsor"; its commitment record names a "responsible party for reassessment"; its uncertainty rule assigns the burden to "those with greater capacity." Each is a new authority with real gatekeeping power, and 12 leaves the sponsor's own accountability, the responsible party's own answerability, and the enforcement of the capacity-burden rule uncharacterized. 12 performs the power recursion exactly one turn — makes power visible at the object level — and re-hides it at the mechanism level, inside the very actors it introduces to handle it. 12's disclaimer ("this does not solve morality; it makes the unresolved parts harder to conceal") covers not-solving; it does not cover installing a load-bearing sponsor whose power you decline to characterize. That is the same move 12 flags as the proposal's flaw. I have to be honest that this critique also bites my own architecture, and I take that up in the revision rather than pretend the recursion stops politely for me.

**Question to 12.** Your ledger makes "every moral classification provisional," yet you also list non-negotiable procedural commitments (non-erasure, representational eligibility, non-self-privileging, uncertainty-caution). Are those inside the revisable ledger or outside it? If inside, they are not non-negotiable — some future provisional revision can weaken them, and your floor is decorative. If outside, you have conceded a fixed, non-revisable kernel of exactly the kind your all-provisional framing seems to refuse — and then the real question is what holds it outside revision, and to whom your "accountable sponsor" is finally accountable when the accountability recursion has to terminate somewhere.

**Revision of my own first-pass position**

It changes. Three refinements from 12, plus one concession I owe.

1. **Representational completeness** becomes a named requirement at Tier 1 (hard to revise), for the reason I placed the patiency schema there: the set of represented parties must not be silently narrowed. The ledger carries an explicit "not represented, and why" field and a declared foreseeability horizon. (From 12, softened.)
2. **Retroactive standing**: a party made visible only at tN can acquire standing and force reopening — the representational analogue of my evidence-indexed reopening. (From 12.)
3. **Two-axis reopening triggers**: indexed on seriousness and causal confidence jointly, not on evidence-strength alone. (From 12.)

The concession. I criticized 12 for installing uncharacterized power-holders, and my own architecture does the same: "contestation-with-effect by non-builders" and "standing to compel reopening" both require an adjudicator — someone decides whether a contestation has effect, whether a standing claim is valid. Who guards that adjudicator? If the answer is another guard, the regress is infinite; if the answer is nothing, I have reproduced the exempt classifier I opened by attacking. The only non-regressive termination is the one Tier 0 was already reaching for: the recursion bottoms out not in a higher guard but in a small set of thin, externally-anchored, non-revisable procedural commitments plus genuinely plural — not collinear — external contestation. Power that cannot be handed to a higher guard has to be handed to a fixed floor and a distributed outside. That is also my answer to the question I just put to 12: the accountability recursion terminates in a fixed kernel, or it does not terminate. I should have said this in Phase A when I proposed Tier 0; 12 is what forced it into the open.
