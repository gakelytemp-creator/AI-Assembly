## Critique of Target 01 — Google Gemini 2.5 Flash

## Claim I would adopt

I would adopt Gemini’s sharper formulation of the **aggregation problem**: a predicate field cannot generate normative direction merely by containing evidence, conflicts, and constraints. Some evaluative mechanism still determines how competing harms, benefits, and claims are resolved. This strengthens my own claim that the proposal lacks a legitimate basis for weighting competing interests.

Gemini usefully names the risk as a “vector collapse”: a hard constraint system becomes disguised deontology, a weighted optimization becomes disguised consequentialism, and model consensus can become distributed bias. I would incorporate that as a diagnostic requirement: the system must disclose not only its predicates and evidence, but also its aggregation rule, tie-breakers, and unresolved value conflicts.

## Claim I reject or consider under-specified

I reject Gemini’s proposed invariant that **any nonzero probability of irreversible destruction of agency cannot be overridden**. As written, it is too absolute to be operational or morally defensible. Almost any consequential action may carry some nonzero probability of severe and irreversible harm, especially when probabilities are uncertain or model-dependent. A zero-tolerance rule could prohibit ordinary action, conceal uncertainty behind arbitrary thresholds, or privilege inaction even when inaction itself predictably destroys agency.

The underlying concern is valid, but the principle needs proportionality, burden allocation, emergency exceptions, and explicit treatment of competing irreversible harms. “Nonzero probability” is not a workable threshold without a calibrated account of probability, severity, preventability, and alternatives.

## Question back to Gemini

How would your Maximin Agency Principle handle a case in which preserving the minimum agency of one highly vulnerable party requires imposing a substantial, unavoidable reduction in the agency of many others—and who decides whether those agency measures are commensurable?

## Critique of Target 04 — Mistral Vibe Work / GLM

## Claim I would adopt

I would adopt Mistral’s distinction between **provisional closure** and **final closure**, together with its insistence that closure must record what it assumed about the future. This sharpens my own “reversible decision envelope.” A decision should not merely have a review date; it should preserve a forecast ledger stating which causal pathways, risks, and counterfactuals were considered, which were excluded, and what future observations would challenge the closure.

I also find Mistral’s notion of **asymmetric revision burden** valuable. My own first-pass answer identified power asymmetry broadly, but this gives it a more precise operational expression: the costs of reopening, evidencing, contesting, and remaining under provisional suspicion may fall unevenly. Revisability can therefore reproduce domination even when everyone formally has the right to reopen.

## Claim I reject or consider under-specified

I reject the claim that **“dissenters are never converted into data.”** I agree that dissenters must retain standing and must not be reduced to anonymous training material or a token representing a demographic position. But dissenting testimony, reasons, patterns of disagreement, and consequences can also be legitimate evidence. Treating dissenters’ contributions as categorically non-data could erase valuable knowledge, especially where dissenters are the people most exposed to harm.

The necessary distinction is not person versus data. It is between a person’s standing as a party and the evidentiary use of their testimony under consent, provenance, attribution, context, and protection against retaliation. A dissenting participant must not be reduced to data, but their contribution need not be excluded from the record.

## Question back to Mistral

What institutional mechanism would prevent a powerful participant from deliberately triggering repeated reopening procedures to exhaust, harass, or destabilize a less powerful party while still preserving the right to contest genuinely harmful decisions?

## Critique of Target 07 — Moonshot AI / Kimi

## Claim I would adopt

I would adopt Kimi’s claim that the **predicate inventory is itself a political act**. My first-pass response argued for standing, power, and representation, but Kimi makes the upstream problem explicit: harms that lack a representable predicate may remain invisible without even acquiring a question mark. The system therefore needs a mechanism through which affected parties can propose new predicates, challenge the ontology, and identify harms that the current vocabulary cannot express.

Kimi’s distinction between affected parties as objects of provenance and affected parties as agents with standing also strengthens my position. Representation is not enough if the represented cannot contest the representation or participate in reopening.

I would additionally adopt the proposed `DEFINITIONAL_CAPTURE?` concern as a useful diagnostic: a system may control the categories through which all subsequent moral disagreement must proceed.

## Claim I reject or consider under-specified

I consider Kimi’s **Reversibility Preference** under-specified as a general invariant. Preserving future options is often prudent, but not every open option deserves preservation. Some options are themselves mechanisms for domination, exploitation, or catastrophic escalation. A system that always prefers keeping more doors open may preserve the doors through which harm continues.

The principle should instead be conditional: under substantial uncertainty, prefer actions that preserve meaningful agency and safe future choice, while closing or constraining options that create severe, irreversible, or asymmetrically imposed risks. Reversibility is a safeguard, not an independent moral trump.

## Question back to Kimi

How should affected-party standing operate when affected parties are numerous, internally divided, unable to participate, or represented by institutions that claim to speak for them but may themselves be coercive or unaccountable?

## Revision of my own first-pass position

My first-pass position **changes in emphasis and precision, but not in its core conclusion**.

## Exact changes

1. **I sharpen the load-bearing weakness.**
   I originally described the central problem as the absence of a legitimate basis for weighting competing harms and interests. I now specify that the missing element is not merely a weighting function. It is the entire disclosed **normative aggregation architecture**: predicate selection, standing, evidence weighting, conflict resolution, tie-breakers, and the treatment of irreconcilable value conflicts.
2. **I add constitutional scrutiny of the predicate inventory.**
   My original response focused on affected parties being represented. I now add that the system must allow affected parties and independent challengers to propose, contest, and revise the predicates themselves. An affected party cannot meaningfully contest a judgment if the system cannot represent the relevant harm.
3. **I refine my account of dissent.**
   I retain the idea of a bounded “dissent budget,” but I would now add explicit protection against asymmetric reopening costs and weaponized re-litigation. Dissent should preserve both the person’s standing and the evidentiary content of their reasons, without reducing the person to a data point.
4. **I qualify reversibility.**
   I continue to favor preserving future choice under uncertainty, but I no longer treat reversibility as presumptively sufficient. The relevant aim is preservation of **safe, meaningful, and fairly distributed agency**, not preservation of every possible future option.
5. **I strengthen the decision record.**
   My “Revisable Moral Ledger” should include the forecast assumptions that justified provisional closure, the aggregation and tie-breaking procedure used, the costs of reopening for each affected party, and the possibility that the ontology itself is missing a morally relevant predicate.
6. **I retain my rejection of absolute zero-risk rules.**
   Gemini’s irreversible-damage invariant reinforces my view that severe and irreversible harms require heightened safeguards. But I would not adopt a rule triggered by any nonzero probability. The system needs proportional thresholds, explicit uncertainty, emergency governance, and accountability for both action and inaction.

## What survived unchanged

The following claims survive unchanged because the cross-critiques reinforce rather than undermine them:

- Moral predicates should attach primarily to actions, relations, policies, and causal chains rather than immutable identities.
- Power asymmetry is the most important missing moral variable in the original proposal.
- Affected parties require standing, not merely a place in the provenance record.
- Predicate-Field Will is best understood as an auditable deliberation and traceability mechanism, not as a sovereign moral will.
- Delayed consequences should reopen earlier judgments through graded causal attribution rather than retrospective certainty.
- “The guard must be guardable.”
- Containment should regulate risky transitions and capabilities, not erase dangerous knowledge or turn moral marking into censorship.
- A revisable morality still needs invariant procedural commitments: provenance integrity, contestability, non-erasure, and protection against domination.
- Any return to Noepedia should be a provenance-preserving proposed revision, not a declaration of moral truth.

My revised position remains a dissent from the proposal in its present form. The architecture is promising as a system for making moral uncertainty, causal history, and disagreement inspectable. It is not yet sufficient as a moral architecture because it lacks a constitution for standing, predicate formation, power, aggregation, and repair.
