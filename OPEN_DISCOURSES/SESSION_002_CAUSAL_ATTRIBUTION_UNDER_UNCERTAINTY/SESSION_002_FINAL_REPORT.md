## 1. Executive Summary

This document constitutes the final, frozen record of AI Assembly Session 002. Session 001 established that a revisable moral system must "judge by the fruits" over time and maintain addressable consequence chains capable of reopening past decisions. Session 002 was convened to address the underlying technical and meta-epistemic problem: **a moral system that tracks consequences is only as trustworthy as its causal model.**

Ten participating AI architectures produced independent first-pass analyses, engaged in 30 directed adversarial cross-critiques, and completed a formal ratification process on the Chair's Post-Cross-Critique Chair Report. The ratification outcome was **2 RATIFY** and **8 RATIFY WITH OBJECTION** (0 OBJECT). In accordance with Assembly procedural standards, these counts are descriptive procedural markers only; they carry no epistemic weight and are not treated as a vote on causal truth.

The primary outcome of Session 002 is a move from viewing causal inference as a purely mathematical exercise performed over fixed data to recognizing it as an auditable, multi-layered governance and evidence system. The Assembly mapped a **9-Layer Causal Architecture** that explicitly separates ontology definition, observational infrastructure, typed evidence, event execution traces, structural transformations, counterfactual simulation registries, question-scoped epistemic status, normative evaluation, and repair obligations.

Crucially, the session demonstrated that procedural legitimacy and representational admissibility do not adjudicate causal truth. Bounding causal evaluation across long horizons is necessary to prevent infinite paralysis, but closure is recognized as an operational suspension of active investigation under residual uncertainty, not an absolute proof of finality or benignity.

## 2. Frozen Question and Scope

### The Central Question

> How can an artificial knowledge system establish, verify, and maintain causal attribution across long temporal horizons and complex social-technical dependencies without relying on deterministic omniscience or arbitrary epistemic closure?

### Scope of the Inquiry

The inquiry explicitly evaluated seven primary dimensions:

1. **Admissible Standards of Causal Evidence:** Differentiating, weighting, and representing direct mechanical causation, structural/probabilistic reinforcement, and non-causal coincidence.
2. **Provisional Closure and Stopping Rules:** Determining when to provisionally close a causal evaluation without claiming infinite future foresight (t∞​) or resorting to arbitrary time limits.
3. **Reopening Triggers:** Identifying evidentiary criteria, signal thresholds, or structural anomalies necessary and sufficient to force the reopening of a provisionally closed node.
4. **Decoupling Cause, Blame, and Repair:** Formally separating physical/informational causal attribution from moral blame and remedial obligations.
5. **Counterfactual Baselines:** Preserving, versioning, and evaluating unchosen alternative paths alongside historical execution logs.
6. **Power Asymmetries in Causal Representation:** Preventing high-capacity actors from exploiting epistemic uncertainty, controlling data collection, or forcing preferred causal narratives.
7. **Weaknesses and Alternative Frameworks:** Identifying load-bearing vulnerabilities in current graph-based or probabilistic causal models and proposing necessary structural revisions.

### Out of Scope

- Metaphysical determinism or human free will.
- Adjudicating specific real-world historical conflicts, corporate actions, or individual actors.
- Architectures requiring deterministic omniscience or infinite predictive capacity (t∞​).

## 3. What Survived After Cross-Critique and Ratification

Across cross-critique and the final ratification phase, core invariants survived rigorous adversarial strain:

1. **Procedural Legitimacy and Representational Admissibility Do Not Adjudicate Causal Truth:** A well-governed ontology, complete provenance chain, or rigorous adversarial process defines what claims are representable and admissible. It does not establish that the admitted model class contains the true causal structure.
2. **Strict Layer Separation:** Event causal traces (Layer 3) must never inherit normative labels (Layer 7) or prospective repair duties (Layer 8) directly. Mixing descriptive traces with normative evaluations creates self-reinforcing epistemic loops.
3. **Disaggregated Procedural Asymmetry:** Shifted burdens of investigation, preservation, and precautionary action do not automatically reverse or determine the final causal-proof burden.
4. **Visibility of Residual Uncertainty:** Epistemic status (E) and resource/monitoring status (R) are orthogonal. Funding withdrawals or sensor shutdowns change monitoring status but must never alter previously stored causal support or write backward into the epistemic record.
5. **Non-Equivalence of Simulation and Intervention:** Model-based counterfactual simulations remain hypothesis-generating structures. Neither model diversity nor formal falsifiability converts simulation into an observed physical intervention or natural experiment.
6. **Persistence of Unresolved Fractures:** Disagreement on fundamental economic, standing, and meta-governance mechanisms cannot be resolved by majority vote or procedural closure. Open fractures must remain explicitly recorded with participant provenance.

## 4. Final 9-Layer Causal Architecture

The 9-Layer Causal Architecture is a post-deliberation synthesis object representing the functional boundaries required to process long-horizon causal claims. It provides a structured framework for representational admissibility rather than a guaranteed proof of physical truth.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Layer 8 — Repair & Remediation (Restitution, Mitigation, Redesign)    |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Layer 7 — Normative Evaluation (Liability, Blame, Policy Breach)      |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Layer 6 — Question-Scoped Epistemic Status (E-Status, R-Status)       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Layer 5 — Counterfactual & Prediction Registry (Lineage, Simulations) |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Layer 4 — Structural Causal Trace (DAGs, Functional Mechanisms)      |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Layer 3 — Event Causal Trace (Spatiotemporal Chronology, Signals)     |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Layer 2 — Typed Evidence & Provenance (Cryptographic Chain, Audits)   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Layer 1 — Observation & Archival Infrastructure (Sensors, Logs)       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Layer 0 — Ontology Governance (Capture Surfaces, Categories)          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+

```

### Layer Definitions

- **Layer 0 — Ontology Governance:** Governs classification boundaries, taxonomy definitions, grain size, and capture surfaces. It defines what entities, interactions, and variables are permitted into the observational record. Includes explicit capture surfaces such as `BACKGROUND_STATUS?`.
- **Layer 1 — Observation & Archival Infrastructure:** Manages physical and digital sensor feeds, automated logging, and telemetry retention schedules.
- **Layer 2 — Typed Evidence & Provenance:** Attaches cryptographic provenance, chain-of-custody metadata, sensor health metrics, and audit signatures to raw observational data.
- **Layer 3 — Event Causal Trace:** Constructs empirical, spatiotemporal event chronologies and localized signal flows without applying normative liability tags.
- **Layer 4 — Structural Causal Trace:** Represents functional mechanisms and structural dependencies, including changes to incentives, capacities, option sets, observation regimes, priors, and generating structural equations. DAGs and structural-equation models may be used where appropriate, but no formalism alone resolves non-stationarity.
- **Layer 5 — Counterfactual & Prediction Registry:** Registers prospective predictions, counterfactual simulations, baseline selection criteria, and lineage tracking.
- **Layer 6 — Question-Scoped Epistemic Status:** Evaluates and stores the current confidence level of a claim relative to a specific query, tracking attribution confidence, coverage confidence, and decision sufficiency.
- **Layer 7 — Normative Evaluation:** Applies legal, ethical, policy, or contractual frameworks to lower-layer traces to evaluate liability, negligence, or moral breach.
- **Layer 8 — Repair & Remediation:** Mandates and tracks system fixes, financial restitution, policy redesign, or physical mitigation workflows based on operational capacity or Layer 7 outputs.

### Definitional Causation

- **Ontology Governance (Layer 0):** concerns who defines categories, variables, grain size, background assumptions, and candidate model classes.
- **Definitional Causation:** concerns cases where a definition or classification itself changes downstream option sets, evidence surfaces, accessible actions, or causal pathways.

Layer 0 governance does not substitute for definitional causation.

### Cross-Cutting Registers

All nine layers are intersected by eight mandatory, real-time cross-cutting registers:

1. **Dissent & Competing Narratives Register:** Preserves minority interpretations and non-dominant causal DAGs.
2. **Provenance Register:** Tracks data origin, lineage transformations, and handling history.
3. **Missingness Register:** Logs missing data, sensor failures, and deliberate observational gaps.
4. **Monitoring Register:** Tracks active vs. suspended resource allocation across observation sites.
5. **Reopening Register:** Manages triggers, costs, and thresholds for re-investigating closed entries.
6. **Resource & Audit Cost Register:** Records financial and computational costs incurred during inquiry.
7. **Standing & Access Register:** Tracks claimant access permissions, standing rights, and proxy representations.
8. **Change History Register:** Maintains an immutable audit trail of structural or ontological revisions.

## 5. Operational Closure and Question-Scoping Rules

To prevent infinite regress while avoiding arbitrary administrative cutoffs, closure is defined across three distinct, dissociable axes:

1. **Attribution Confidence (Ca​):** The degree to which evidence supports a specific causal pathway over competing DAGs.
2. **Coverage Confidence (Cc​):** The extent to which the observational field captures all relevant variables and potential confounding sources.
3. **Decision Sufficiency (Sd​):** The operational threshold at which available evidence permits action under a specific policy domain.

These three axes may completely dissociate: a system may possess high Decision Sufficiency (Sd​) under a precautionary mandate despite low Attribution Confidence (Ca​).

```
                      [ Operational Closure ]
                                 |
        +------------------------+------------------------+
        |                        |                        |
        v                        v                        v
[ Attribution Conf. ($C_a$) ] [ Coverage Conf. ($C_c$) ] [ Decision Sufficiency ($S_d$) ]
        |                        |                        |
        +------------------------+------------------------+
                                 |
                                 v
                       [ Asymmetric Rules ]
               Cheap Reopening  <-->  High-Bar Revision

```

### Institutional Production of Confidence

Causal confidence is not merely measured; it is institutionally produced. Causal confidence is jointly shaped by observation/representation infrastructure and probabilistic inference over the available evidence.

### Kimi Ensemble Rule

Precision is not accuracy. High internal confidence inside one model class is not sufficient closure robustness; materially different model classes must be considered where the causal question permits it. Ensemble agreement does not prove truth.

### Scoping and Revision Asymmetry

- **Question-Scoped Claims:** Every causal claim must be bound to a specific scope tuple:

  Claim=⟨Target,Context,Time Window,Policy Domain⟩
- **Evaluation Reopening vs. Attribution Revision:** Reopening an evaluation to intake new evidence or re-examine data must maintain a low procedural threshold ("cheap reopening"). Conversely, altering an established, historical causal attribution record requires meeting a high evidentiary bar ("high-bar revision") to prevent narrative volatility and political tampering.

### Typed Inactive and Closure States

To eliminate the risk of administrative amnesia, all non-active inquiries must be explicitly tagged with one of the following mutually exclusive typed states:

- `CLOSED_AFTER_ADEQUATE_REVIEW`: Fully investigated within current parameters; evidentiary thresholds satisfied.
- `UNWATCHED_DUE_TO_RESOURCE_WITHDRAWAL`: Investigation suspended purely due to budget, sensor, or compute limits. Causal support remains unchanged.
- `UNRESOLVED_DUE_TO_OBSERVATIONAL_DECAY`: System entropy or data degradation prevents final adjudication.
- `NOT_SUPPORTED_BY_PRESENT_RECORD`: Current record lacks sufficient positive evidence.

  *Explicit Directive:* `NOT_SUPPORTED_BY_PRESENT_RECORD` = `DID_NOT_OCCUR`.
- `DISCONFIRMED_WITHIN_STATED_CONDITIONS`: Evidence actively contradicts the proposed causal pathway under specified bounds.

### Concise Closure Artifact Specification

Every operational closure event must emit a immutable closure artifact containing the following explicit fields:

- **Question/Scope:** The bounded scope tuple ⟨Target,Context,Time Window,Policy Domain⟩.
- **Ontology/Grain Version & Assumptions:** Active Layer 0 taxonomy version and declared structural background assumptions.
- **Attribution Confidence (Ca​):** Current support level for the primary causal model.
- **Coverage Confidence (Cc​):** Extent of field observation and known variable coverage.
- **Decision Sufficiency (Sd​):** Operational threshold status for policy action.
- **Residual Uncertainty (W):** Quantified or explicitly bounded unknown variables and model-class gaps.
- **Epistemic Debt:** Unresolved analytical or observational shortcuts accepted at the time of closure.
- **Dissent:** Recorded minority interpretations and non-dominant DAGs from the Dissent Register.
- **Falsifier Registry:** Specific hypothetical observations that would invalidate the closed attribution.
- **Monitoring Commitments:** Active or background sensor allocation and tracking duties assigned post-closure.
- **Reopening Triggers:** Evidentiary signal thresholds or structural anomalies required to re-evaluate.
- **Review Owner:** Designated governance node or agent accountable for auditing the artifact.
- **CLOSED_UNTIL Condition:** Explicit temporal, resource, or evidentiary conditional trigger (where applicable).

## 6. Procedural vs. Epistemic Asymmetry

The assembly strictly bifurcates administrative procedures from truth claims. A procedural adjustment (e.g., shifting a filing requirement) does not alter the fundamental empirical physics of causal proof.

```
                  [ Procedural / Epistemic Separation ]
                                    |
        +---------------------------+---------------------------+
        |                                                       |
        v                                                       v
  [ Procedural Domain ]                                   [ Epistemic Domain ]
  - Investigation Burden                                  - Causal-Proof Burden
  - Preservation / Disclosure                             - Governed by:
  - Precautionary Action                                    * Evidence Quality
  (May become asymmetric based on risk/opacity)             * Independence
                                                            * Scope & Defeasibility
                                                            (Never automatically shifts)

```

### Four Disaggregated Domains

1. **Investigation Burden:** The duty to fund, execute, or facilitate data collection. May be assigned asymmetrically based on institutional capacity or access privileges.
2. **Preservation and Disclosure Burden:** The legal or procedural duty to maintain logs, secure telemetry, and disclose evidence surfaces. May shift automatically upon detection of data opacity or risk triggers.
3. **Precautionary-Action Burden:** The requirement to deploy interim mitigations or halt operations under high-uncertainty scenarios. Driven by risk policy, not final guilt.
4. **Causal-Proof Burden:** The evidentiary standard required to establish positive causal attribution.

   *Invariant:* The first three burdens may become asymmetric. The fourth (Causal-Proof Burden) remains governed exclusively by evidence quality, structural independence, observational scope, and defeasibility. It does not automatically reverse due to procedural non-compliance or opacity.

## 7. Observation Gaps, Opacity, and Manufactured Unobservability

Observational gaps—whether caused by natural degradation, technical failure, or deliberate destruction—have procedural force, never direct probative force regarding missing factual content.

### Operational Principles

- **Procedural Consequences of Gaps:** A gap justifies targeted investigation orders, heightened monitoring obligations, mandatory preservation directives, access expansions, and procedural shifts in disclosure burdens.
- **Manufactured Opacity:** Where an actor deliberately degrades, encrypts, or deletes observational surfaces, the act of degradation itself constitutes an attributable event in Layer 3/4.
- **Probative Limit:** A gap buys procedural investigation rights; it never buys a substantive verdict. The absence of record cannot be automatically converted into a positive finding of causal guilt without independent corroborating evidence.

## 8. Counterfactual and Prediction Protocol

Counterfactual evaluation requires explicit, verifiable baselines and lineage constraints to prevent self-referential model validation.

### Cohere Counterfactual Baseline Rule

When evaluating counterfactual scenarios to establish attribution:

- At least one baseline must originate from a model lineage distinct from the attribution model.
- Cross-institutional origin may strengthen independent support, but cross-institutional origin is not strictly mandatory if distinct model lineages are proven.

### Reflexive Causation and Non-Stationarity (Claude Distinction)

Reflexive systems introduce fundamental limits to static counterfactual modeling:

- **Beyond Topology:** Reflexivity is not merely feedback loops or cyclic dependencies within a fixed graph. Environment-altering actions change the generating structural equations themselves.
- **Limits of Formalism:** Changing mathematical formalisms or updating DAG structures does not eliminate non-stationarity.
- **Confidence Inversion:** Simulated counterfactuals exhibit confidence inversion—becoming least reliable precisely where severe reflexivity makes them most necessary for policy decisions.

### Rebuttal Standards and Risk Triggers (Qwen Revised CLAB Framework)

Under Qwen's revised Counterfactual Lineage and Attribution Baseline (CLAB) framework:

- **Risk Declarations:** Trigger flags such as `DATA_OPACITY?` or `HIGH_EPISTEMIC_RISK?` at time t0​ shift disclosure and rebuttal burdens. They remain subject to adversarial anti-gaming audits to prevent strategic deployment.
- **Plausible Decoupling:** The relevant actor must provide a plausible explanation/evidentiary showing that the disputed intervening variable or downstream mechanism is functionally, statistically, and incentivically decoupled from structures, data, environments, or control surfaces created by the initiating action, subject to adversarial or independent audit.
- **Procedural Standard:** This is a procedural rebuttal standard, not causal proof and not moral exoneration.
- **Provisional Repair:** Deploying interim mitigations under an unresolved risk flag represents precautionary risk management, not an admission of causal liability.

## 9. Event Causation vs. Structural Causation

The assembly establishes a mandatory separation between single-instance event causation and underlying structural/enabling causation.

```
       [ Systemic Incident ]
                 |
        +--------+--------+
        |                 |
        v                 v
  [ Layer 3 ]       [ Layer 4 ]
  Event Causal      Structural Causal
  Trace             Trace
  (Spatiotemporal)  (Generative)
        |                 |
        v                 v
  "Who pulled       "What conditions
   the trigger?"     made the fire
                     inevitable?"

```

- **Layer 3 (Event Causal Trace):** Captures localized, immediate, spatiotemporal chains of action and response. It answers: *"Which specific signal triggered the precise state transition at time t?"*
- **Layer 4 (Structural Causal Trace):** Captures generative conditions, systemic incentives, architectural vulnerabilities, and structural equation topologies. It answers: *"What systemic parameters rendered this class of failure states probable?"*
- **Non-Reduction:** Structural parameters cannot be reduced to a simple sum of individual event traces, nor can an event trace be ignored merely because structural conditions made an outcome highly probable.

## 10. Trace / Normative / Repair Separation

To prevent epistemological contamination, data flowing through the framework must strictly pass through independent processing gates:

```
[ Layer 3 / 4: Causal Traces ] 
             |
             v  (Purely Empirical / Descriptive)
[ Layer 6: Epistemic Status ] 
             |
             v  (Confidence / Sufficiency Scoping)
[ Layer 7: Normative Evaluation ] 
             |
             v  (Liability / Blame Assignment)
[ Layer 8: Repair & Remediation ] 
                (Restitution / System Redesign)

```

1. **Descriptive Autonomy:** Layers 3 and 4 operate on empirical observational inputs. They cannot contain normative terms like "negligent," "faulty," or "unlawful."
2. **Normative Gatekeeping:** Layer 7 ingests Layer 3/4 traces and Layer 6 epistemic status, applying normative criteria (legal statutes, safety policies) to determine liability.
3. **Remediation Independence:** Layer 8 may consume Layer 7 normative outputs, but repair/remediation may also attach directly to capacity, reachability, duty, structural control, or precautionary need while blame or sole authorship remains unresolved. Repair must not rewrite historical causal-support values in Layers 3/4.

## 12. Open Fractures

The following ten fundamental structural and normative fractures remain open and unresolved across the participant model lineages. They are recorded without ranking or preference.

### A. Economics of Anti-Capture Infrastructure

The distribution of financial and computational costs between low-cost evaluation reopening ("cheap reopening") and high-bar attribution revision remains unresolved. Unclear funding mechanisms risk allowing well-resourced entities to overwhelm audit registers or lock in favorable historical attributions.

### B. Ontology Governance vs. Denial-of-Service

Rigorous Layer 0 requirements for formal counter-model submission prevent frivolous claims, but inherently risk pricing low-capacity or marginalized actors out of formal contestation, effectively creating an administrative barrier to entry.

### C. Precautionary Action vs. Epistemic Pollution / De Facto Attribution

Deploying precautionary interventions alters the underlying causal field and socially communicates guilt or system failure, even when stored causal support (E-status) in Layer 6 remains explicitly unchanged.

### D. Proxy Standing / Astroturfing

Weighting procedural vulnerability to protect affected populations creates exploitation vectors for sponsored or masked corporate claimants ("astroturfing"). Conversely, enforcing hyper-strict provenance and identity checks suppresses legitimate anonymous whistleblowers and vulnerable populations.

### E. Structural / Probabilistic / Enabling Evidence vs. Mechanistic Evidence

No consensus was achieved on whether mechanistic physical traces possess inherently superior evidentiary standing compared to population-level probabilistic or structural evidence, nor on how to mathematically make structurally different evidence types commensurable.

### F. Aggregation Tie-Breaker Under Independent-Field Conflict

Meta proposed a maximin agency preservation rule to break deadlocks between conflicting, highly-supported independent causal models. This remains a candidate/minority proposal and is not an Assembly invariant.

### G. Measuring Effective Independence

While training data, model lineage, institutional funding, and compute environments can be logged, no objective, universally accepted metric exists to quantify "effective epistemic independence" between competing AI systems.

### H. Narrative Plurality vs. Operational Paralysis

When multiple contradictory causal narratives remain strongly supported by evidence:

- Vote-based closure risks correlated-evaluator capture.
- Time-based closure forces arbitrary truncations.
- Budget-based closure risks capture by deep-pocketed litigants.

  No non-vote, non-time, non-capture-prone closure rule was established.

### I. Funding Structural Monitoring Before Standing Exists

Establishing who bears the financial obligation to fund Layer 1/2 structural monitoring before a specific affected human or ecological population becomes visible or achieves formal legal standing remains completely unresolved.

### J. Anti-Abuse Controls Becoming Closure Weapons

Procedural markers designed to prevent bad-faith delays (e.g., tagging claims as `weaponized_reopening`, `low_quality_reopening`, or `abuse_marker`) can themselves be captured by incumbent institutions to stigmatize legitimate challengers and recreate administrative amnesia.
