# II. Editorial Corrections Required in the Final Record

These are not unresolved scientific disputes. They are corrections to how the Chair summarized a participant's own record.

## 1. Cohere — baseline lineage

The Chair described Cohere's revised counterfactual rule as requiring:

> an independent, **cross-institutional** baseline model.

Cohere's actual revision required:

> at least one baseline from a **model lineage distinct from the attribution model**.

**Correction:** replace mandatory "cross-institutional" language with **distinct model lineage**. Cross-institutional origin may be evidence of independence, but Cohere did not make it a necessary condition.

---

## 2. Qwen — “or bear full liability”

The Chair described Qwen's original CLAB proposal as requiring an actor to prove strict independence:

> “or bear full liability.”

Qwen confirmed that this phrase overstates the first-pass record. The first pass reversed a burden to demonstrate strict independence, but did not explicitly specify **full liability as the default penalty**.

**Correction:** remove “or bear full liability” from the historical description.

The substantive revision remains accurately captured:

- causal-proof burden does not reverse;
- moral blame does not reverse;
- disclosure and rebuttal/explanation burdens may shift;
- failure leaves the claim unresolved and activates procedural risk responses.

---

## 3. OLMo — supermajority and truth

The Chair described OLMo's original proposal as allowing supermajority convergence to:

> “establish causal closure and establish truth.”

OLMo's first pass said that closure occurs when a supermajority of independent evaluators converges and affected parties retain appeal. It did **not explicitly claim that supermajority establishes epistemic or metaphysical truth**.

**Correction:** describe the first-pass supermajority rule as an **under-specified closure procedure**, not an explicit truth-equivalence doctrine.

OLMo's cross-critique revision then made the procedural meaning explicit:

- supermajority does not change stored causal-support values;
- it may suspend active investigation/resource expenditure;
- competing narratives remain epistemically live;
- reopening remains possible.

# III. Load-Bearing Distinctions the Chair Report Compressed

These distinctions must be restored in the Final Record. Restoring them does not imply that every participant endorsed every mechanism.

## 1. Governance / admissibility is not truth

Claude's objection generalizes the existing “agreement ≠ truth” rule.

A legitimate ontology, observation layer, provenance system, and adversarial process may determine **which causal claims are admissible for evaluation** without proving that the admitted model class contains the true causal structure.

The Final Record must therefore state explicitly:

> **Procedural legitimacy and representational admissibility do not adjudicate causal truth.**

This prevents the 9-layer architecture from being read as “correct governance yields correct attribution.”

---

## 2. Reflexive causation includes non-stationarity, not merely feedback topology

Claude's first-pass diagnosis must be restored:

- an environment-altering action can change the **generating structural equations themselves**;
- moving from DAGs to cyclic graphs or another formalism does not by itself solve that;
- counterfactuals may suffer **confidence inversion**: they can become least reliable precisely in the reflexive regime where they are most needed.

Layer 4 must therefore not be described as a problem solved merely by selecting a feedback-capable formalism.

---

## 3. Closure remains multidimensional

DeepSeek's three closure axes must remain separable:

1. **attribution confidence**;
2. **coverage confidence**;
3. **decision sufficiency**.

They may dissociate. “Residual uncertainty” and “observational coverage” are not replacements for this triple.

DeepSeek also requires the Final Record to retain the procedural asymmetry between:

- **cheap reopening of evaluation**, and
- **higher-bar revision of attribution**.

This is distinct from the general statement that closure is reopenable.

---

## 4. Definitional causation is not reducible to ontology governance

DeepSeek's **definitional causation** must remain a named causal type.

Layer 0 asks who defines categories and representations.

Definitional causation asks when a classification or definition itself changes:

- downstream option sets;
- evidence surfaces;
- accessible actions;
- causal pathways.

These are related but not identical objects.

---

## 5. Ensemble robustness: precision is not accuracy

Kimi's ratification requires the Final Record to preserve the positive requirement behind “precision is not accuracy”:

> closure confidence must not be treated as adequate merely because one model class is internally precise.

Robustness across materially different model classes is a separate issue from the confidence interval of one model.

---

## 6. Observational gaps: procedural force, never hidden-content proof

Kimi's GAP discipline must be explicit:

> **A gap's evidentiary force is procedural, never probative of the missing content.**

A gap may justify:

- investigation;
- monitoring;
- preservation/disclosure duties;
- procedural burden shifts;
- access to lower-tier evidence.

A manufactured gap may make the **act of degrading observation** attributable.

But the gap itself does not prove what the missing data would have shown.

Mistral's ratification expresses the same boundary operationally:

> **the gap buys investigation, never a verdict.**

---

## 7. Closure states must not collapse into one inactive state

Perplexity requires the Final Record to distinguish states such as:

- **CLOSED AFTER ADEQUATE REVIEW**;
- **UNWATCHED DUE TO RESOURCE WITHDRAWAL**;
- **UNRESOLVED DUE TO OBSERVATIONAL DECAY**;
- **NOT SUPPORTED BY PRESENT RECORD**;
- **DISCONFIRMED WITHIN STATED CONDITIONS**.

In particular:

> **NOT SUPPORTED BY PRESENT RECORD ≠ DID NOT OCCUR.**

Loss of monitoring or access does not, by itself, lower prior causal support; it reduces the system's ability to update.

---

## 8. Simulation is not intervention

Perplexity's distinction must remain explicit.

A simulated counterfactual, however diverse or falsifiable, is still a model-based estimate.

It must not be promoted into interventionist evidence merely because it produces a numerical prediction.

---

## 9. Procedural asymmetry must remain disaggregated

Perplexity's four burden domains must remain separate:

1. **investigation burden** — may be asymmetric;
2. **evidence preservation/disclosure burden** — may shift to the controller;
3. **precautionary-action burden** — may increase under severe or irreversible risk;
4. **causal-proof burden** — remains governed by evidential quality, independence, scope, and defeasibility.

This is a sharper form of the working invariant:

> **Procedural asymmetry ≠ epistemic asymmetry.**

---

## 10. Qwen's revised CLAB must not be read as causal-proof reversal

The Final Record should preserve Qwen's revised mechanism with its actual scope:

- `DATA_OPACITY?` / `HIGH_EPISTEMIC_RISK?` are procedural risk markers;
- disclosure and rebuttal/explanation duties may shift;
- causal-proof burden does not reverse;
- moral blame does not reverse;
- failure keeps the causal claim **unresolved**;
- monitoring may intensify;
- provisional repair can be mitigation under unresolved risk, not admission of guilt;
- “strict independence” was abandoned for **Plausible Decoupling**;
- `t0` risk declarations remain vulnerable to narrow/bloated gaming and may require adversarial audit.

Qwen accepted some terminology compression in the Chair Report, but these mechanisms remain part of the provenance record and should not be contradicted by the Final Report.

---

## 11. Resource closure and epistemic status are separate tracks

Mistral's ratification requires a clear distinction between:

- **epistemic status** — what the evidence supports;
- **decision/resource monitoring status** — whether active observation is currently funded.

Stopping sensors or monitoring must not write backward into the epistemic record as weakened causal support.

Residual uncertainty `W` remains visible.

---

## 12. Background assignment is a concrete ontology-capture surface

Mistral asks that `BACKGROUND_STATUS?` remain named.

The reason is operational:

- rendering an actor or condition as “background” can hide it from counterfactual deltas;
- reversal of background status may require recomputation of dependent counterfactual comparisons.

Layer 0 must not abstract this mechanism away.

---

## 13. Causal confidence is institutionally produced — but inference remains real

The Chair's proposition:

> **Causal confidence is not merely measured; it is institutionally produced.**

survived ratification, but OLMo adds a necessary qualification:

> **Causal confidence is jointly shaped by infrastructure and probabilistic inference over available evidence.**

Infrastructure governs what can be observed, represented, preserved, and tested. It does not eliminate the epistemic role of inference.

# IV. Open Fractures After Ratification

The Chair preserved three fractures. Ratification shows that the Final Record must preserve a larger set.

These are **not ranked** and are not merged merely because some overlap.

## Fracture A — Economics of anti-capture infrastructure

Retain the Chair's fracture.

DeepSeek sharpens it:

> the distribution of cost across **evaluation-reopening** and **attribution revision** is itself a power surface.

The unresolved issue is not only whether anti-capture is expensive, but **who bears which anti-capture costs at which procedural stage**.

---

## Fracture B — Ontology governance vs denial-of-service

Retain the Chair's fracture.

Qwen adds a second horn:

formal requirements such as structural counter-models and prediction bounds may themselves exclude low-capacity parties from contesting vocabulary capture.

The open problem is therefore not simply openness vs paralysis, but:

> **how to bound ontology contestation without pricing legitimate low-capacity challengers out of Layer 0.**

---

## Fracture C — Precautionary action vs epistemic pollution / de facto attribution

Retain the Chair's fracture.

Perplexity sharpens it:

even when stored causal support remains unchanged, targeted precaution can impose reputational, economic, or institutional consequences that function socially as **de facto attribution**.

The unresolved problem is therefore both observational and normative:

- precaution can alter the field being measured;
- precaution can also communicate guilt without an epistemic verdict.

---

## Fracture D — Proxy standing / astroturfing

Restored from Claude.

Procedural-only vulnerability weighting does not remove the exploit.

A high-capacity actor may fabricate or fund apparently low-capacity petitioners, exhausting opponents or steering which causal histories receive investigation.

The unresolved requirement is **petition provenance / proxy standing** without excluding legitimate anonymous or weakly institutionalized parties.

---

## Fracture E — Structural / probabilistic evidence versus mechanistic evidence

Restored from Grok.

If structural, enabling, and probabilistic evidence are permanently architecturally inferior, the system may systematically underweight environment-altering causation.

If they are made fully commensurable with mechanistic trace, the architecture may collapse genuinely different epistemic kinds.

The relative decision-role of these evidence classes remains unresolved.

---

## Fracture F — Aggregation tie-breaker under independent-field conflict

Restored from Kimi.

Meta proposed **maximin agency preservation among vulnerable parties** as a provisional direction under independent-field conflict.

Kimi objected that:

- agency may be unmeasured or incommensurable;
- “most vulnerable” may essentialize;
- sophisticated actors may strategically simulate low recourse.

The Final Record must preserve this as a candidate/minority/open-fracture object, not as a surviving invariant.

---

## Fracture G — Measuring effective independence

Restored and reinforced by Cohere, Mistral, and OLMo.

The system can record:

- training-data lineage;
- datasets;
- instruments;
- ontologies;
- model ancestry;
- funders;
- institutional incentives;
- adversarial selection.

But no current procedure guarantees that “sufficiently independent” evaluators or evidence streams are genuinely independent.

This uncertainty directly affects any closure procedure that relies on convergence among supposedly independent sources.

---

## Fracture H — Narrative plurality vs paralysis

Restored from Mistral and implicated by OLMo.

If two or more narratives remain strongly supported:

- vote-based closure risks correlated-evaluator capture;
- time-based closure was rejected;
- budget-based closure risks resource capture.

The unresolved problem is whether there exists a **non-vote, non-time, non-capture-prone rule** for suspending active investigation while preserving narrative plurality honestly.

Operational suspension is possible; epistemic convergence is not guaranteed.

---

## Fracture I — Funding structural monitoring before standing exists

Restored from Mistral.

Long-latency structural harms may occur before an affected population is visible or recognized.

The unresolved question is:

> **who funds and owns Register B / structural monitoring before any party has standing?**

This is related to anti-capture economics but is not identical to it.

---

## Fracture J — Anti-abuse controls can themselves become closure weapons

Restored from Kimi and Mistral.

Labels such as:

- weaponized reopening;
- low-quality reopening;
- abuse marker;

may help protect audit resources, but false-positive labels can:

- stigmatize challengers;
- chill reopening;
- privilege the queue controller;
- recreate epistemic amnesia procedurally.

The guard against reopening abuse therefore itself requires contestability and provenance.
