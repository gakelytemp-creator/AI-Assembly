## C. Mandatory corrections still required

### 1. Restore the exact Frozen Question

The candidate report replaces the frozen question with:

> “How can distributed, non-stationary, and structurally opaque systems perform accountable causal attribution without collapsing into epistemic capture, procedural paralysis, or reified institutional bias?”

That is a new editorial paraphrase and must not replace the frozen question.

Restore exactly:

> **How can an artificial knowledge system establish, verify, and maintain causal attribution across long temporal horizons and complex social-technical dependencies without relying on deterministic omniscience or arbitrary epistemic closure?**

The paraphrase may appear only as explanatory prose after the frozen question, if desired.

### 2. Restore definitional causation explicitly

The candidate Final Report contains ontology governance but omits **definitional causation** as a named causal type.

The Final Report must distinguish:

- **Ontology Governance:** who defines variables, categories, grain, background, model classes;
- **Definitional Causation:** when a classification/definition itself changes downstream option sets, evidence surfaces, accessible actions, or causal pathways.

Layer 0 does not substitute for definitional causation.

### 4. State “precision is not accuracy” as an operational rule

Kimi's ensemble requirement appears only in the Seeded/Emergent genealogy.

It must also appear in the operative architecture or closure section:

> **Precision is not accuracy. High internal confidence inside one model class is not sufficient closure robustness; materially different model classes must be considered where the causal question permits it.**

Do not imply that ensemble agreement proves truth.

### 5. Restore the causal-confidence qualification

The candidate report omits the ratified paired statement.

> **Causal confidence is jointly shaped by observation/representation infrastructure and probabilistic inference over the available evidence.**

Infrastructure affects what can be observed and represented; it does not eliminate the epistemic role of inference.
