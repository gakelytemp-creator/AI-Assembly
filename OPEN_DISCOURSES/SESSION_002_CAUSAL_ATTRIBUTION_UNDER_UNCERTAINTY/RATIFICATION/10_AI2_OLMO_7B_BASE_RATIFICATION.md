## 2. Material objection or correction

The Chair Report slightly overstates the original supermajority proposal by summarizing it as establishing "causal closure and establish truth." In my original position, I did not claim that supermajority establishes epistemic truth, but rather that it signals provisional closure of active investigation with explicit reopening triggers and affected-party notification. My revision explicitly rejected any implication that supermajority equals truth. The Chair should clarify that the original proposal was under-specified and not intended as truth-equivalence, and that the revision explicitly restricts supermajority to procedural resource allocation.

Additionally, the Chair states, "Causal confidence is not merely measured; it is institutionally produced," but omits my qualification that confidence is jointly produced by infrastructure and probabilistic inference over available evidence. This nuance should be preserved to avoid misreading infrastructure control as negating the role of inference.

## 3. One unresolved disagreement that must remain in the Final Record

Whether "sufficiently independent" evaluators can ever be reliably measured remains unresolved. While provenance, lineage, and diversity metrics can be tracked, no method guarantees independence in practice, especially when institutional incentives and training data overlap subtly. This creates a persistent risk that procedural closure could still mask epistemic capture, even under the revised framework.
