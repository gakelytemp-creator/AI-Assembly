## 2. Material objection or correction

The Chair Report omits a load-bearing distinction in my closure framework. “Closure is a recorded suspension of active investigation under residual uncertainty” is broadly compatible with my position, but it should not collapse all non-active states into one category. The Final Record should preserve distinct epistemic statuses:

**`CLOSED AFTER ADEQUATE REVIEW UNWATCHED DUE TO RESOURCE WITHDRAWAL UNRESOLVED DUE TO OBSERVATIONAL DECAY NOT SUPPORTED BY PRESENT RECORD DISCONFIRMED WITHIN STATED CONDITIONS`**

In particular, `NOT SUPPORTED BY PRESENT RECORD` must explicitly remain distinct from `DID NOT OCCUR`. Resource withdrawal, degraded observation, or missing access do not reduce the prior causal support of a link; they enlarge or preserve uncertainty and may weaken the system’s ability to update it.

The report should also explicitly preserve the distinction between **simulation and intervention**. A simulated counterfactual is a model-based estimate, not interventionist evidence merely because it forecasts downstream change. Model diversity and forward falsifiability are safeguards, but they do not transform simulation into an observed intervention or natural experiment.

Finally, the procedural-versus-epistemic distinction should remain disaggregated across four domains: investigation burden, evidence-preservation/disclosure burden, precautionary-action burden, and causal-proof burden. The first three may legitimately become asymmetric under power, vulnerability, severity, irreversibility, or control of records. The causal-proof burden must remain epistemically governed by the evidence’s quality, independence, scope, and defeasibility—not by claimant status, consensus, reputation, or moral seriousness.

## 3. One unresolved disagreement that must remain in the Final Record

The unresolved joint is whether targeted precaution before causal proof can remain genuinely non-attributive in practice. A precautionary restriction, monitoring regime, disclosure duty, or resource shift may be warranted under serious uncertainty, but it can impose reputational, economic, and institutional consequences that function as de facto attribution even if the stored causal-support value remains unchanged.

The Final Record should preserve this as more than a generic “precautionary action versus epistemic pollution” problem. It concerns the practical boundary between an evidentiary label and the real-world effects of acting on it: a system can maintain formal epistemic discipline while still imposing harms that communicate “we have effectively decided you caused this.”
