## 2. Material objection or correction

The Chair Report contains one material understatement and one material merge.

**(a) Understatement — the three closure axes are not preserved as a triple.**

My first-pass specified closure as a multi-axis condition over **attribution confidence**, **coverage confidence**, and **decision sufficiency**. The Chair Report replaces this with a single “question-scoped resource suspension state tied to observational coverage, residual uncertainty, monitoring triggers, and structural-break markers” (§1, Dynamic t_max + epsilon). This is not the same object. “Observational coverage” is not equivalent to *coverage confidence*, and “residual uncertainty” is not equivalent to *attribution confidence*. The three axes were kept separate in my record precisely because they can dissociate: a claim can have high attribution confidence and low coverage confidence (well-supported inference over a surface known to be incomplete), or high coverage confidence and low decision sufficiency (the question is well-instrumented but the answer does not bear on the decision at issue). The Chair Report’s re-description compresses these into a single suspension state. This is a merge, not a preservation.

**(b) Merge — cheap reopening of evaluation vs expensive revision of attribution.**

My first-pass and cross-critique distinguished **reopening evaluation** (cheap, low-bar, procedural) from **reopening/revising attribution** (expensive, higher-bar, requires admissible evidence). The Chair Report preserves typed reopening triggers (§1, §2 Layer 6) and preserves `CLOSED_UNTIL`, but does not preserve the *cost asymmetry between evaluation-reopening and attribution-revision* as a structural feature of the architecture. The Report’s treatment of reopening as “monitored, falsifier-indexed, and reopenable” (§7) does not carry the distinction. This matters because the distinction was load-bearing against the weaponization vector I identified: if both operations are equally priced, low-capacity challengers face the higher price for mere evaluation, and high-capacity actors face no special price for attribution revision — the asymmetry the architecture was meant to correct is reintroduced at the reopening layer.

**(c) Understatement — definitional causation as a distinct tier.**

My first-pass placed definitional causation as Tier D, a distinct evidence/causal type in its own right, and my cross-critique sharpened the criterion (classification becomes mechanistic when it changes the downstream option set or evidence surface). The Chair Report preserves “invariance” and “lineage/dependence” at Layer 2 (§2) and preserves the upstream placement of Ontology Capture (§1), but does not preserve definitional causation as an explicit named category. The Chair’s Layer 0 placement is compatible with my position but does not substitute for it: Layer 0 governs *who authors definitions*; definitional causation names *when a definition is itself doing causal work in the trace*. These are related but distinct, and the Final Record should not let the first stand in for the second.

No material objection exists to the following: the narrowing of vulnerability to procedural domains (§1); the rejection of scalar ε and fixed t_max (§8); the rejection of same-model counterfactual validation (§8); the rejection of supermajority truth-equivalence (§8); the rejection of absence-of-evidence-as-evidence-of-absence (§8); the preservation of the trace → normative → repair separation (§1); or the preservation of Layer 0 as strongly supported.

## 3. One unresolved disagreement that must remain in the Final Record

**The economics of anti-capture infrastructure (Chair’s Fracture 1) must remain in the Final Record, and it must be sharpened to include the reopening-cost asymmetry I named in §2(b) above.**

The Chair frames Fracture 1 as “anti-capture machinery is necessary but may itself privilege actors who can afford it.” That framing is correct but incomplete for my record. The unresolved joint is not merely that anti-capture infrastructure has costs; it is that the *distribution of those costs across reopening evaluation vs attribution revision* determines whether the architecture’s power-asymmetry correction survives its own procedural layer. My first-pass held that evaluation-reopening should be cheap and attribution-revision expensive; if the Final Record does not fix this asymmetry explicitly, then whatever anti-capture infrastructure is built can be priced such that low-capacity actors cannot afford evaluation-reopening while high-capacity actors can afford repeated attribution revision. The disagreement is unresolved because neither I nor the Chair has specified who bears the cost of the evaluation-reopening path, or what mechanism prevents that cost from being shifted onto the parties the architecture is meant to protect. It cannot be averaged away: the two framings (“anti-capture is expensive” vs “anti-capture cost distribution is itself a power surface”) point to different design commitments.
