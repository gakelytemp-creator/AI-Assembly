# IV. Cross-Critique Round 1 — what survived attack

The cross-critique synthesis distinguishes:
**SURVIVED / REVISED / DID NOT SURVIVE IN ORIGINAL FORM / OPEN FRACTURE**.

## A. SURVIVED

### 1. Ontology governance is upstream of inference
The strongest surviving architectural shift.

Claude: modeling-choice problem.
DeepSeek: ontology-authority problem.
Grok: explicit Layer 0.
Mistral: `BACKGROUND_STATUS?` widened into ontology-authoring protocol.
OLMo: revised Narrative Arena to include ontology governance.

Surviving principle:

> **A causal system must record and expose how the world was carved before it evaluates causal links inside that carving.**

Variables, node boundaries, grain, candidate pathways, background conditions, option sets, model classes, and data-collection regimes are contestable objects.

No formalism swap alone solves vocabulary capture.

### 2. Causal evidence remains typed/multidimensional
The scalar-confidence model did not survive.

Surviving dimensions/types include:
- mechanistic/direct;
- difference-making/probabilistic;
- structural reinforcement;
- enabling/precondition;
- definitional causation;
- invariance;
- provenance;
- coverage;
- dependence;
- missingness/access.

A key durable distinction:

> **Event causation** and **structure causation** are different claims.

“A caused E” differs from “A changed S to S′ under which events like E became more probable.”

### 3. Provenance and independence are causal evidence
Plurality is not independence.

Dependence may arise through shared:
- datasets;
- instruments;
- funders;
- priors;
- model lineages;
- institutions;
- ontologies;
- evaluator ancestry.

Evaluator independence must be measured, not merely asserted.

### 4. Closure is procedural/provisional, not a truth event
Strongest surviving meaning:

> **Closure is suspension of active investigation under declared conditions and residual uncertainty.**

It does not mean disproved, benign, universally resolved, or forgotten.

A closure artifact may contain:
- exact question/scope;
- ontology/grain version;
- evidence state;
- missingness;
- residual uncertainty;
- dissent;
- falsifiers;
- monitoring;
- reopening triggers;
- review owner;
- `CLOSED_UNTIL`.

DeepSeek sharpened:
> **the node is a site; the question is the unit of closure.**

### 5. Missingness is first-class, but not proof of hidden content
Kimi's strengthened scheme:
- GAP-0 ordinary missingness;
- GAP-1 contested-location gap;
- GAP-2 actor-linked/manufactured gap.

Invariant:

> **A gap may license investigation, monitoring, disclosure/preservation duties, or risk flags. It never proves what the missing data contained.**

Mistral further revised reopening:
a specific custodian-attributable `OBSERVATIONAL_GAP?` may substitute for evidence novelty for the **right to investigation**, but not for causal claim strength.

### 6. Causal trace / normative evaluation / repair remain separate
This survived all direct pressure.

Key firewall:

> **Procedural or moral reasons may change what the system investigates or does; they do not automatically change what causal evidence means.**

Repair may attach to capacity/reachability/duty/control even when blame or sole authorship is unresolved.

### 7. Counterfactuals survive only as plural, provenance-bearing, defeasible objects
Surviving safeguards:
- multiple baseline families;
- assumptions explicit;
- provenance;
- model-lineage diversity;
- forward falsifiability;
- empirical counterfactuals where possible;
- adversarial/shadow baselines;
- explicit option-set definition;
- disagreement across baselines preserved.

Same-model baseline can be auxiliary, not independent validation by itself.

### 8. Reflexive causation requires structural-change representation
No single formalism won.

But participants preserved the need to distinguish:
- ordinary event propagation;
- changes to incentives, capacities, observation regimes, option sets, priors, structural equations, or other causal-generating rules.

Downstream agency does not automatically sever upstream structural contribution; upstream contribution does not absorb all downstream agency.

# V. Major proposals that survived only after material revision

## 1. `t_max + epsilon`
Original: confidence/time-based closure.
Result: a lone scalar/time rule was rejected universally.
Time may remain an input, but closure must expose observation coverage, falsifiers, structural breaks, missingness, resource conditions, and question scope.

## 2. Vulnerability-weighted reopening
Original: lower threshold based on vulnerability/low capacity.
Pressure: proxy standing, astroturfing, identity gatekeeping, lack of sensor access.
Result: asymmetry moved primarily to investigation/monitoring/preservation/disclosure/precaution, not stored causal-support values.

## 3. Cohere reputation weighting
Original: predictive reputation could increase epistemic authority.
After critique: reputation was re-scoped to procedural priority/triage/audit allocation only.
It does not directly strengthen causal-support values.

## 4. Cohere same-model counterfactual
Original: same causal model could generate attribution and baseline.
After critique: CAM added model-lineage IDs and requires at least one independent baseline lineage.
Same-model baselines remain auxiliary.

## 5. Qwen burden reversal
Original: initiator must prove later harm was “strictly independent.”
After critique:
- causal-proof burden does not reverse;
- moral-blame burden does not reverse;
- disclosure and rebuttal/explanation burdens may reverse;
- failure activates epistemic-risk/data-opacity flags, preserves unresolved status, and may intensify monitoring;
- “strict independence” became **plausible decoupling** under independent/adversarial review.

## 6. Mistral novelty-priced reopening
Original: novelty/lineage independence/mechanism specificity determine cheap reopening.
After critique:
- novelty still affects claim strength;
- novelty is no longer necessary for investigation rights;
- a specific, custodian-attributable observational gap may substitute for novelty in the investigation track.

## 7. OLMo supermajority closure
Original: causal closure after supermajority convergence of independent evaluators.
After critique:
- supermajority does not alter causal-support values;
- it does not establish causal truth;
- it may only suspend active investigation or allocate resources procedurally;
- competing narratives remain epistemically live;
- if evaluator dependence is high, supermajority is weak evidence.

This is one of the clearest author-side retreats in the round.
