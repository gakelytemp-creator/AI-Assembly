## Own‑Position Revision

### Exact changes to CAM architecture

1. **Closure logic** – replaced scalar confidence threshold with a **triple condition**: (a) epistemic uncertainty ≤ θ, (b) resource‑cost condition (information gain < audit cost), (c) falsifier‑indexed monitoring active. The global horizon H is now a **composite signal** of epistemic, resource, and anomaly components, not a simple average.
2. **Evidence aggregation** – added **provenance‑dependence vector** requiring at least two evidence types with independent provenance before a claim can achieve high confidence. Cross‑verification is mandatory for closure.
3. **Counterfactual generation** – modified to store **model lineage IDs** for each baseline. The system now enforces **baseline diversity**: at least one baseline must come from a model lineage distinct from the attribution model. Same‑model baselines are permitted only as auxiliary, never as the sole independent reference.
4. **Reputation weighting** – retained but **re‑scoped**: reputation influences only procedural priority (e.g., triage, audit allocation) and does **not** alter the stored causal‑support value of a claim. Reputation is updated based on verified prediction accuracy, not self‑reported confidence.
5. **Global averaged horizon H** – retained but **redefined**: H is now a **dynamic composite** that aggregates epistemic, resource‑cost, and anomaly signals, each with its own weight. The previous simple average is abandoned.
6. **Falsifier registry** – added a versioned registry of pre‑registered falsifiers per claim, with active‑monitoring status and expiry conditions. Closure requires that all listed falsifiers are being monitored.
7. **Provenance‑dependence vector** – each claim now carries a vector recording shared datasets, instruments, funders, model priors, and evaluator lineages, enabling an estimate of effective independence.

### Surviving disagreement

- I continue to reject **scalar confidence thresholds** as sufficient closure criteria; thresholds remain necessary operational tools, but their assumptions, costs, and error distributions must be visible and contestable.
- I continue to reject **supermajority closure** as a truth condition; agreement may reflect shared dependencies, not independent corroboration.
- I continue to reject **same‑model counterfactuals** as the sole independent baseline; at least one independent lineage is required.

### Cross‑target collision

All three targets expose a common tension: **procedural efficiency versus epistemic integrity**. Qwen’s resource‑bounded rule, OLMo’s supermajority, and Claude’s falsifier indexing each attempt to manage closure or revision costs, but each can be subverted if the underlying evidence or evaluator independence is compromised. The collision forces a design where procedural mechanisms (resource budgeting, supermajority, falsifier monitoring) are **isolated from epistemic weighting**; the former guide *when* to invest effort, the latter determine *what* the evidence means. This separation is now explicit in CAM.
