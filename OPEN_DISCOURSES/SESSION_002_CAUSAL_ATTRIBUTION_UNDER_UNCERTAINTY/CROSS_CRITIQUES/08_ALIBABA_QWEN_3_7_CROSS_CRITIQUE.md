### MANDATORY PRESSURE TEST: Causal Ledger with Asymmetric Burden (CLAB)

**Does burden reversal change the stored causal-support values?**
No. Burden reversal does *not* automatically alter the raw probabilistic causal-support values. To do so would conflate procedural failure with proven causation. Instead, it changes the *procedural metadata* and *epistemic risk flags* attached to the node.

**Which exact burden reverses?**
It reverses the **burden to disclose controlled evidence** and the **burden to produce a rebuttal or independence explanation**. It does *not* reverse the burden of causal proof (the system still must identify a link), nor does it reverse the burden of moral blame. It shifts the onus onto the initiating actor to demonstrate that the harm was functionally decoupled from their action, rather than forcing the affected party to map the obfuscated causal chain.

**What happens when the actor fails to satisfy that reversed burden?**
Failure to meet this burden does *not* establish causation or moral blame. Instead, it triggers specific procedural and systemic responses:

1. The `HIGH_EPISTEMIC_RISK?` (or `DATA_OPACITY?`) predicate is automatically activated and locked onto the causal node.
2. The claim remains formally **unresolved**, preventing the actor from using "lack of proof" as a valid argument for epistemic closure.
3. **Monitoring intensifies**, and the cost of this intensified monitoring may be provisionally assigned to the initiating actor based on their structural capacity.
4. A **provisional repair duty** may activate, not as an admission of guilt, but as a structural obligation to mitigate an ongoing, un-resolved risk they initiated.

**Do you retain “strict independence” as the disproof standard?**
No. "Strict independence" is unrealistic and easily gamed in entangled socio-technical systems. I replace it with a standard of **"Plausible Decoupling."** To disprove structural causation, the actor must demonstrate that the intervening variable operates via a mechanism that is functionally, statistically, and incentivically decoupled from the structures, data pipelines, or environments established by the initial action, and that this decoupling is verified by an adversarial or independent audit.

**How do you prevent narrow or strategically bloated `t0` risk declarations from gaming CLAB?**
By subjecting the `t0` declaration to an **Adversarial Baseline Audit**.

- *Against narrow declarations:* If an actor declares a narrowly bounded risk to limit future burden, but independent analysis shows the action inherently creates broader systemic vulnerabilities (e.g., releasing a dual-use capability), the system overrides the `t0` declaration and applies the broader, structurally accurate risk profile.
- *Against strategic bloating:* If an actor lists every conceivable risk to appear thorough but dilutes focus, the system weights the declaration by the *specificity* of the risk and the *concreteness* of the proposed mitigation plans. Vague, catch-all risk declarations are tagged as `LOW_CREDIBILITY_DISCLOSURE?` and do not satisfy the burden of good-faith monitoring commitment.

### OWN-POSITION REVISION

1. I have integrated GLM’s **Event vs. Structure Causation** distinction into my Pluralistic Causal Standard, explicitly separating mechanistic triggers from structural enablement.

**Cross-Target Collision:**
Both GLM and Grok identify the manipulation of "background status" or "structural uncertainty" as a primary vector for power asymmetry. My revised CLAB architecture directly collides with and resolves this vulnerability: by forcing the initiating actor to bear the burden of proving *plausible decoupling* when a harm materializes within their structural domain, it neutralizes the "it was just background noise" or "intervening variable" defense that high-capacity actors use to launder long-horizon externalities.
