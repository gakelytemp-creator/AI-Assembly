### Own-position revision

Yes, the position changes.

**Exact changes**

1. Elevate ontology governance (who selects variables, definitions, data regimes, and model classes) to an explicit, contestable Layer 0 inside Track A of the dual-track ledger; this layer carries its own provenance, residual conflict, and power markers.
2. Replace the original single list of reopening triggers with four typed triggers: mechanical/pathway, distributional/prediction-gap, standing-based, and model-inadequacy/structural-change.
3. Adopt typed evidence classes that remain distinct objects (mechanistic trace, structural reinforcement, enabling condition, etc.) and store a vector of support values rather than a single scalar; this supplies the technical basis for distinguishing structural responsibility from event responsibility.
4. Require every provisional closure to be a typed artifact that records forecast assumptions, predicted fruits, option-value impact, epistemic debt, missingness ledger, and review ownership—explicitly separating epistemic confidence from decision-relevant confidence.
5. Incorporate a missingness ledger and evidence-access asymmetry predicates as first-class elements that can force independent preservation or disclosure obligations without automatically converting absence into affirmative causation.
6. Strengthen the epistemic firewall: the causal-trace layer may not embed normative predicates (HARM?, etc.) in its variable definitions; normative evaluation and remediation remain downstream and independently contestable.

**Surviving disagreement**

I continue to reject any architecture in which Tier B (structural/probabilistic) evidence is permanently barred from contributing to decision-relevant attribution solely because it cannot be upgraded to Tier A pathway status. In reflexive social-technical systems, many of the most consequential effects are structural; treating them as architecturally second-class would systematically under-weight the very environment-altering causation the session correctly identifies as central.

**Cross-target collision**

Target 03’s Contested Causal Ledger treats ontology governance (Layer 0) as prior to and non-derived from the causal trace, while Target 05’s Reflexive Causal Field and Target 06’s Causal Claim Dossier treat power and access asymmetries as assessments that sit alongside or inside the evidence and model layers. These cannot both be accepted without an additional distinction: either ontology decisions are constitutively prior (and therefore require their own independent contestation regime) or they are themselves causal claims subject to the same evidence standards as any other link. The collision is unresolved by simple conjunction.

**Pressure-test note (structural vs. event responsibility; epistemic vs. decision-relevant confidence)**

Structural responsibility (enabling conditions, incentive and capacity shifts, definitional capture) can and should be represented inside the causal-trace layer as distinct typed objects without importing normative judgment; the normative layer then evaluates those objects. Decision-relevant confidence can diverge from pure epistemic confidence when the record explicitly flags (a) the remaining epistemic debt, (b) the seriousness-weighted cost of false negative versus false positive, and (c) the monitoring commitment that accompanies provisional action. The divergence remains non-normative in the trace layer so long as seriousness and cost are recorded as inputs to the decision rule rather than as modifiers of the causal strength values themselves. If seriousness is allowed to alter the stored causal-support numbers, the firewall collapses.
