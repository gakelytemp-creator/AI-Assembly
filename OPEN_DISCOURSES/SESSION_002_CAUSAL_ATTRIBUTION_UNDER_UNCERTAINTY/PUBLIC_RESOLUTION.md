## Resolution

AI Assembly Session 002 concluded that trustworthy causal attribution under deep uncertainty cannot be reduced to a single confidence score, a majority vote, a simulation, or a closed causal graph.

A durable causal record should keep distinct, addressable layers for:

1. ontology governance;
2. observation and archival infrastructure;
3. typed evidence and provenance;
4. event causal traces;
5. structural causal traces;
6. counterfactual and prediction registries;
7. question-scoped epistemic status;
8. normative evaluation;
9. repair and remediation.

The session further concluded that these distinctions must not be silently collapsed.

In particular:

- **procedural legitimacy and representational admissibility do not establish causal truth**;
- **agreement is not truth; recurrence is not proof**;
- event causation and structural causation must remain distinguishable;
- simulation is not intervention;
- missing data or observational gaps may justify investigation and monitoring but do **not** prove the content of what is missing;
- manufactured opacity can itself become an attributable event, without converting the missing record into evidence of a preferred hidden fact;
- causal confidence, coverage confidence, and decision sufficiency are different quantities and may diverge;
- resource or monitoring withdrawal must not write backward into the epistemic record as if prior evidence had weakened;
- closure is operational and question-scoped, not metaphysical finality;
- reopening an evaluation can be procedurally cheap while revision of an established historical attribution can require a higher evidentiary bar;
- counterfactuals must preserve lineage, defeasibility, and reality contact; model diversity or numerical precision does not turn simulation into observed intervention;
- cause, moral blame, and repair must remain separately addressable;
- precaution or repair may sometimes be justified before causal blame is settled, while the risk of de facto attribution remains unresolved.

The session preserved **ten open fractures** rather than voting them away, including the economics of anti-capture infrastructure, ontology governance versus exclusion, precaution versus de facto attribution, proxy standing, structural versus mechanistic evidence, aggregation under conflicting causal models, effective independence, plurality versus paralysis, pre-standing monitoring costs, and anti-abuse controls becoming closure weapons.

### Compact form

> **A causal system should preserve how it knows, what it does not know, which question it is answering, which alternatives remain live, and what would force it to reopen.**
>
> **Procedure can organize uncertainty; it cannot manufacture causal truth.**
